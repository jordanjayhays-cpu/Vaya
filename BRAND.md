# Vaya brand book, v1

2026-09-06. The source of truth for how Vaya looks, talks, and what it never does. The designed version is published as an artifact ("Vaya Brand Book"); this file is the text. Taglines and manifesto are offered in three versions for Jordan to pick.

## The idea

Tourism boards sell you a postcard. We send you a correspondent. Before you move to a city, visit it properly, or put money into it, you ask one question: what's it really like? Vaya is a local newsroom in every city worth moving to, telling the truth on camera, and then the services that handle what the truth revealed.

- **The name.** Vaya means "go" in Spanish. It also spells Voices Around Your Area. Said before a city, it's an invitation: Vaya Manila. Go find out.
- **The promise.** Every episode answers a real question with real numbers from a real person who lives there. Every service exists because someone we filmed had the problem.
- **The stance.** Not a travel brand. Not a news brand. A city, told by the people in it, that you can book. Warm because it's honest, not instead of it.

## Who we talk to

One person with one question, never a category. Four of them: the two-week visitor (often a returning Filipino), the retiree, the corporate mover, the month-stayer.

## Voice, five rules

1. **First-hand.** Someone who lives there says it.
2. **Specific.** The neighborhood, the price, the nurse's name, the time of night. A number beats an adjective.
3. **Honest, including the bad.** Traffic, safety scores, the visa that doesn't exist.
4. **Warm, not hype.** Enthusiasm is shown by the detail, not the adjective.
5. **Short.** One idea per sentence. If it could be in a brochure, cut it.

The test for every line: would the friend who already moved say this over a beer? Makati, not Manila. The number, not the vibe. Her name, not "a nurse."

| Not Vaya | Vaya |
|---|---|
| Discover the vibrant heart of Manila's nightlife scene. | Poblacion at 11pm: what a drink costs, where to go, and when to leave. |
| Affordable healthcare makes the Philippines a retiree paradise. | Maricel is a licensed nurse. She earns PHP 32,000 a month. In Ohio the same care is US$6,000. Both numbers on screen. |
| Seamless relocation services tailored to your needs. | Someone's at arrivals with your name. The SIM is in the car. GCash is set up by Makati. |
| Manila is safe if you take normal precautions. | Makati scores 62 on safety. Manila as a whole scores 35. Here's why we say the neighborhood, not the city. |
| Unlock the digital nomad lifestyle with the new visa. | The nomad visa was signed in April 2025. It still doesn't exist. Here's what you can actually do for 36 months. |
| Cheap help is one of the perks of living here. | A live-in caregiver costs a fifth of Toronto, and she's paid above Manila market rate. Here's her contract. |

**Never use:** vibrant, hidden gem, paradise, unlock, seamless, bustling, authentic, cheap labor, third world, exotic, nomad-friendly, curated experience.
**Use:** here's the number, what it actually costs, who it's for and who it isn't, the neighborhood name, the person's name, filed, handled, we checked, we don't know yet, go find out.

## Naming: Vaya, then a plain noun

- **Cities:** Vaya Manila, Vaya Cebu. The city is the invitation.
- **Services:** Vaya Care, Vaya Nights, Vaya Line, Visa Desk, Landing, the Vaya Month. Vaya plus what it does. Never coined words.
- **Series:** no Vaya prefix. Title case, three words or fewer: Block by Block, The Real Cost, Eat Here Not There, After Dark, The Read, A Week In, 48 Hours in Manila, Coming Home, Retire in Manila.
- **Newsletter:** One City, Unpacked.

## Tagline, three to pick from

- **Safe:** The friend who already moved. (Warm and true. A sentence, not a stamp.)
- **Bold:** Go find out. (What vaya means, as an instruction. Needs the brand around it for the honest half.)
- **Weird:** Manila, actually. (Per city. The word before a truth. Reads as a correction, which is the point and the risk.)

## Manifesto, three versions, sixty seconds each

**Safe.** You've asked the question. What's it actually like there? And you've had the answers: a brochure, a clip, a forum thread from three years ago. We're the other answer. People who live in the city, telling you what it costs, what it's like, whether you'd be happy. With the numbers on screen. And when the truth shows you the hard parts, we do those for you. Vaya. The friend who already moved.

**Bold.** Nobody sells you a city honestly. The tourism board wants your visit. The developer wants your deposit. The influencer wants the view count. We want you to know. So here's the rent, by building. The safety score, by street. The nurse's salary, next to yours. The visa that doesn't exist yet. Then here's someone at arrivals with your name. Vaya. Go find out.

**Weird.** Manila is not for you. Unless it is. Unless you have a reason: a mother in Quezon City, a job in BGC, a pension that buys a nurse here and a bus pass at home. Then it's the best city you've been told to avoid. We live here. We'll show you the receipt. Manila, actually.

## Disclosure: Vaya is the critic and the salesman

Vaya reviews businesses and sells access to those same businesses. No publisher template covers this, so here is the rule.

**Say the relationship out loud, on camera, in the same breath as the recommendation.** Not in the description, not in fine print. Three cases, three lines:

| The relationship | What is said |
|---|---|
| **On a Vaya route, we earn** | "Full disclosure: this place is on our food tour. If you book through us, we make money. We would send you here anyway, and here is why." |
| **No relationship** | "No relationship here. We just eat here." |
| **They gave us something** | "They comped our meal. We told them the review runs either way." |

Supporting rules:

- **We pay our own way.** Meals, rooms, and tours are paid for unless the comp is disclosed on screen. Decide before landing in the city, because deciding in the moment always goes the same way.
- **Nobody pays to be recommended.** Partners are paid to deliver. A business cannot buy a place in a film or on a route.
- **The recommendation comes first, the link second.** Never the other way round.
- **This exists before film one.** A disclosure added at film four makes films one to three look like the ones we hid.

## Signature device: the receipt

Every Vaya film ends on a receipt-style card with the real numbers from what you just watched. Mono type, dotted rules, a total, and the Vaya line last, in red. Numbers only from that episode. Also the format for the newsletter's weekly number and for every quote Vaya sends a customer.

## Look

- **Theme:** Hibiscus. Paper #FFFFFF, card #FFF7F8, hibiscus #FF2D55, tangerine #FF8A3D, ink #23141B. Dark mode tokens are in the site pages.
- **Type:** Plus Jakarta Sans for everything you read, 800 for headlines with tight tracking, sentence case. DM Mono for labels, prices, receipts, buttons.
- **Wordmark:** "Vaya" in Plus Jakarta Sans 800, tracking minus 4 to 5 percent, followed by a hibiscus full stop. The dot is the logo. Never all caps, italic, or outlined. Never a palm tree, pin, or globe. Sub-brands keep the dot: Vaya Care.
- **Film:** real people named on screen in their own places, daylight and night as they are, no drone-glamour, no golden-hour filters. Prices on screen as they're said. Host in the frame, on the street.

## The film signature

- **Open, 10 seconds:** the question, the place by neighborhood, the number.
- **Body, 8 to 15 minutes:** one real person, one real day or week, facts on screen, the bad parts stay in.
- **The pace rule:** every three seconds something changes. A cut, an angle, a graphic, a punch-in, b-roll, a price tag. Never the same frame for more than three seconds. The receipt lands one row at a time.
- **Close, 15 seconds:** the receipt, one line about the paid thing if it's the honest fix, the newsletter, "Go find out."

## What Vaya never does

1. Never says "Manila" when it means Makati, BGC, or Poblacion.
2. Never makes a worker the punchline. Named, paid fairly, shown being paid.
3. Never recommends on camera what it wouldn't recommend with no deal attached, and never names a business without stating the relationship (see Disclosure).
4. Never takes sponsors. Vaya makes money when someone books. Partners get paid to deliver, never to be recommended, and every partner relationship on screen is disclosed.
5. Never goes to P. Burgos, never sells the go-go bars, never runs a night without a female-guide option.
6. Never films anyone without a signed release, never publishes before they've seen the cut.
