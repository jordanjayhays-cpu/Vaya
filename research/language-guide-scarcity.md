**In the entire directory there is exactly one currently accredited guide per major foreign language, and none at all for German.**

That is not a shortage. That is a market with nobody in it.

## Why this matters more than the food route

`OFFER.md` prices Vaya Tours as a Makati and BGC food walk in English, at PHP 3,000 a head with PHP 1,000 of margin. It competes with every other food tour in the city.

**A Japanese-language Intramuros walk competes with one person.** So does Spanish. So does Mandarin. German competes with nobody at all.

The Philippines receives substantial visitor numbers from Japan, China, Korea and Spain. **Verify the current arrival figures from the Department of Tourism before acting on this**, because the whole idea rests on them. But the supply side is now measured, not guessed, and it is one.

## What it does not mean

**It does not mean the demand is there.** Guides may have let accreditation lapse precisely because the work dried up. That is the first thing to ask, and it is a better question than anything in the current outreach drafts:

> "Your accreditation lapsed in 2024. Was that about the paperwork, or about the work?"

The answer tells you whether this is an opportunity or a graveyard, and it costs one email.

**It also does not mean accreditation is legally required.** Still an open question for the lawyer hour, and it decides whether "expired" is a disqualifier or a formality.

## Data quality problems on the source

Three errors found while reading, all on a government site:

- **Susan Villarama's** accreditation reads `DOT-NCR-TGD-O730-2923`: a letter O for a zero, and the year 2923
- **The Doms Valencia profile** displays Violeta Muñoz's email and mobile number

**Verify any number against the guide before relying on it.** And note that the index page footer says "Updated 12 Feb 2022" while individual profiles carry 2026 dates, so the index is not a reliable guide to currency.
