## The model in one line

Every Manila video is free and answers one question. Every question has one paid thing attached. The video earns trust, the paid thing earns money, and the newsletter connects the two.

Rule from the media research: experiences and services are the business, media is the funnel. Time Out's food halls carry its media. Semafor is half events. Vaya is built that way from day one.

## Sequencing: the first 90 days

**Days 1 to 30. Make the funnel real.**
- Wire the newsletter and Starter Kit forms. Nothing else matters until emails are captured.
- Sign one guide (Venus first) for the food tour and nights. Design the two routes.
- Place affiliate links (eSIM, insurance, coworking) in the Manila page and the Starter Kit.
- Film the first three: cost of a month, where to live, a week in the life.

**Days 30 to 60. First money.**
- Sell the food tour direct on the site. Run it every Saturday.
- Publish the first investor read and put a paid briefing call behind it.
- Approach two brokers and one coliving for referral and in-kind deals.

**Days 60 to 90. Second line.**
- Launch Vaya Nights.
- Sell the first newsletter sponsorship on real numbers.
- Decide whether concierge is a Vaya service or a referral to a partner.

## Rules that protect the brand

- Sponsored content stays under a quarter of output. Vice and BuzzFeed lost audiences past that.
- Every recommendation in a video is one Vaya would make with no deal attached. Affiliate links follow the recommendation, never the other way round.
- Nights: curated venues only, no go-go bars, female-guide option.
- Host-led. The correspondent is the face. Give them a piece of the upside so they don't leave like Vox's hosts did.

## Top 3 ways it goes wrong

1. **Videos before the funnel.** Three films go up, people want the Starter Kit, the form drops the email. Fix: forms first, this week.
2. **Too many lines at once.** Nine rows in a matrix is a menu, not a plan. Fix: 90 days, two lines (experiences, affiliates), then add.
3. **Jordan delivering instead of selling.** Guiding, filming, and concierge are on-the-ground jobs. Fix: Jordan sells and signs partners. The correspondent and the guide deliver.
