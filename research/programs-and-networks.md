# Programs, networks and people, 2026-09-28

Researched: local-news funding, startup programs, IE resources, Philippine incubators.

**The conclusion is uncomfortable and useful. Most of the money is not for you. Two of the best rooms you are already paying for and not using.**

---

# 1. IE, which you are already paying for

You are an IE MBA student for another fifteen months and using none of this.

## Venture Networks: every Thursday, 7pm, Area 31

> "Every Thursday at 7pm in Area 31, you can join in the fun to pitch, brainstorm, and network with potential investors in an informal setting. These sessions are open to students, entrepreneurs, investors, and anyone curious about startups and entrepreneurship."

**This is the Madrid equivalent of Bridgehouse BGC.** A weekly room, free, already yours, and you can start this week rather than in January.

**It is also the cheapest place on earth to practise saying what Vaya is.** You will explain it fifty times to strangers who owe you nothing, and the version that survives is the one that goes on the homepage.

## Venture Lab, the accelerator

IE's startup accelerator, housed in Area 31, **open to all students and alumni and offered as an elective within the International MBA programme.**

**Read that again. It may be a course you can take.** Taking Vaya through your own school's accelerator, for credit, while building it, is the single highest-leverage thing available to you before January.

Contact: **entrepreneurship@ie.edu**

## IE Alumni Philippines Club

**ieconnects.ie.edu/ieaphilippines/home/**

The club's own description of who it is for:

> "former IE students who have a link with or interest in the Philippines; including those who identify as Filipino, those who currently or previously have resided in the Philippines, **those who are exploring opportunities to work, travel or do business in the Philippines**."

That last clause is you, word for word. There is also an **IE Philippine Club** for current students at ieconnects.ie.edu/iephilippine/home/.

**This is the warmest possible introduction to Manila, and it costs an email.** IE people in the Philippines, already disposed to help another IE person, fifteen months before you land. Nothing else in this document comes close on effort-to-value.

**BOARD: join both IE Philippine clubs this week. Go to Venture Networks on Thursday. Ask entrepreneurship@ie.edu whether Venture Lab can be taken as an elective.**

# 2. Local-news funding: almost certainly not for you

This is where the money is, and it is the wrong money.

| Programme | What it is | Why it does not fit |
|---|---|---|
| **Press Forward** | A **US$500 million** US philanthropic project for local journalism | **US local news.** Vaya covers Manila and is foreign-owned |
| **LION Publishers** | US trade body for independent online local news. Received US$4m from MacArthur | Membership may be open, **the money is US-directed** |
| **Tiny News Collective** | Aims to launch 500 new local news organisations | US fiscal sponsorship model |
| **Google News Initiative** | Genuinely global: **US$189m to 6,250 organisations in 118 countries** | **GNI Growth Catalyst closed 7 May 2026.** And it funds existing newsrooms. Vaya has published nothing |

**Do not spend weeks on grant applications you cannot win.** Almost every one of these requires a US-based non-profit newsroom with published output and an audience. Vaya is a foreign-owned Philippine services company with films attached, and zero published.

**What to do instead:** put a calendar note for **early 2027** to check the GNI Innovation Challenge and News Equity Fund. By then you will have published films and a subscriber list, which is the difference between an application and a letter.

**LION membership might still be worth the fee** for the sustainability playbooks and the community, even with no grant attached. Check what it costs and what a non-US member actually gets before paying.

# 3. Philippine startup programmes: not a fit either

**QBO Innovation Hub** is the country's public-private accelerator, built by IdeaSpace, JP Morgan, DOST and DTI. It has deployed a **PHP 2 billion fund to back 200 startups**, and has supported 700+.

**But the fund's focus is deep tech: artificial intelligence, blockchain for supply chains, climate technology.** Vaya is a media and services company. It is not what they are buying.

`research/fdi-philippines.md` already noted the DOST Startup Grant Fund and RA 11337. Same conclusion: **built for technology ventures, not for a newsroom with a concierge desk.**

**Stop treating Philippine startup funding as a route.** It is a distraction from the four products.

---

# 4. What this actually says

**The money programmes want what you do not have** (a US non-profit newsroom, or a deep-tech product) and they will still want it in six months.

**The room programmes want nothing at all**, and you are already inside two of them.

Ranked by effort against value, before January:

1. **Join the IE Alumni Philippines Club and the IE Philippine Club.** One email. Warm Manila contacts fifteen months early
2. **Go to Venture Networks, Thursday 7pm, Area 31.** Free, weekly, and it will sharpen the pitch faster than any document
3. **Ask if Venture Lab can be an elective.** An accelerator, for credit, on the thing you are building anyway
4. **Shoot the three Madrid films** (`briefs/00-madrid-pre-departure.md`). Nothing above matters without published work
5. **Bridgehouse BGC, first Wednesday after landing** (`research/partners-sweep.md`)

Numbers one to three cost one email and one evening a week. **None of them has anything to do with the outreach list, and all of them are better uses of the next month.**
