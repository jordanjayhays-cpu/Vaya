# Care migration: the American care-cost crisis as Vaya's biggest business

Date: 2026-09-13. Prepared after the audience question "who would actually move their whole life to Manila." The answer that survived scrutiny was: the household facing a care bill it cannot pay. This document is the research behind that. Sources inline. UNSURE = no verifiable figure found.

**The thesis in one line:** a US family paying US$115,000 to US$130,000 a year for a nursing home can buy one-to-one licensed care in Manila for a fifth of that, in English, and Vaya is the only company positioned to explain it and then supply it.

## 1. The problem, in American numbers

**What care costs at home (CareScout 2025 Cost of Care Survey, 25,000+ rates collected July to November 2025):**

| Care type | US national median | Per month |
|---|---|---|
| Assisted living | US$74,400 a year, up 5% | US$6,200 |
| Nursing home, semi-private room | US$315 a day, US$114,975 a year, up 2% | US$9,580 |
| Nursing home, private room | US$355 a day, US$129,575 a year, up 1% | US$10,798 |
| Home health aide | US$35 an hour, US$80,080 a year at 44 hours a week | US$6,673 |
| Private duty nurse | US$90 an hour, US$160 a visit | Not comparable |

Sources: [Genworth/CareScout](https://investor.genworth.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1054/carescout-releases-2025-cost-of-care-survey-results), [survey PDF](https://assets.carescout.com/x/8fcb50422f/282102.pdf).

Note the shape of it: a home health aide at 44 hours a week costs almost as much as assisted living, and buys only weekday daytime coverage. **Nobody in America can afford 24-hour one-to-one care.** That is the gap the Philippines closes, and it is a gap of kind, not just of price.

**How many people this is:**

- **56%** of Americans turning 65 today will develop a disability serious enough to need long-term services and support ([SingleCare](https://www.singlecare.com/blog/news/long-term-care-statistics/), [JRC](https://www.jrcinsurancegroup.com/long-term-care-statistics/)).
- Individuals with significant disabilities grow from **7.6M in 2020 to 14.7M by 2065**.
- **63 million Americans are caregivers as of 2025**, up nearly 50% since 2015. One in four adults ([AARP, Caregiving in the US 2025](https://www.aarp.org/pri/topics/ltss/family-caregiving/caregiving-in-the-us-2025/)).
- Family caregiving was worth **US$1 trillion in 2026**, more than all federal, state and local Medicaid spending combined (US$932B in 2024) ([AARP, Valuing the Invaluable](https://www.aarp.org/pri/topics/ltss/family-caregiving/valuing-the-invaluable-2026-update/)).
- **37% of caregivers** say their financial situation got worse because of it.

**The people already voting with their feet:** more than **760,000 Americans collect Social Security abroad**, roughly 60,000 of them in Mexico ([Slate](https://slate.com/life/2026/05/retirement-aging-nursing-home-dementia-cost.html), [The Traveler](https://www.thetraveler.org/americans-look-abroad-for-retirement-as-costs-climb-at-home/)).

## 2. The financing mechanism, which is the actual unlock

This is the part nobody explains and it decides whether a family can move or not.

**Medicare pays nothing.** Original Medicare does not cover custodial long-term care *anywhere*, and provides essentially no benefits outside the United States. Medigap plans C, D, F, G, M and N cover 80% of *emergency* care abroad after a US$250 deductible, capped at US$50,000 lifetime, only in the first 60 days of a trip. That is travel insurance, not care funding ([ElderLawAnswers](https://www.elderlawanswers.com/getting-medicare-while-traveling-or-living-overseas-8229), [InternationalInsurance](https://www.internationalinsurance.com/advice/medicare-while-traveling/)).

**Long-term care insurance is where it turns.** Whether a policy pays abroad depends entirely on its payment structure ([Diversified Quotes](https://www.diversifiedquotes.com/can-you-use-long-term-care-insurance-overseas/)):

| Policy type | Pays overseas? | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| **Indemnity or hybrid** (pays cash on certification of chronic illness) | **Yes, fully** | No geographic restriction, 100% of the benefit pool. Foreign provider licensing is irrelevant because the cash goes to the policyholder. |
| Reimbursement with an international rider | Partially | Typically 6 months to 4 years maximum, often 50% of the domestic daily maximum |
| Reimbursement with no international provision | No | Must return to the US |

And: qualified LTC benefits stay **excludable from federal gross income regardless of where the care happens**, within IRS per diem limits.

**Why this matters more than any other fact in this document.** A retiree with an indemnity or hybrid LTC policy can take the cash to Manila and buy roughly five times the care. A retiree with a reimbursement policy cannot, and does not know it. **"Read your policy's payment structure before you do anything else" is a piece of advice worth thousands of dollars, it takes ninety seconds to give, and no Philippine operator gives it.** That is a film and it is the top of the funnel.

UNSURE: what share of the ~7M US LTC policies in force are indemnity or hybrid versus reimbursement. Find this before sizing the market.

## 3. What the same care costs in Asia

**Philippines, facilities.** Almost no Philippine facility publishes a price; most quote after a geriatric assessment ([liveinph](https://liveinph.com/elder-care-nursing-home-philippines-foreign-retirees)).

| Facility | Location | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Oasis Aged Care Village | Pasay City, Metro Manila | Assisted living PHP 127,000/mo, memory care PHP 132,500, skilled nursing PHP 155,000, private room |
| Life Care | UNSURE (verify location) | From PHP 62,000/mo (about US$1,070) basic assisted living |
| Golden Haven | Cebu | US$800 to 1,500/mo (secondary source, verify) |

**The foreigner surcharge, which nobody warns about.** Published Philippine rates include the Filipino senior-citizen discount under RA 9994. A foreigner without a Filipino Senior Citizens ID pays **+20% plus 12% VAT**, taking Oasis assisted living from PHP 127,000 to roughly **PHP 171,000 (about US$3,000) a month**. Still a third of a US nursing home, but not the number on the website. This is exactly the kind of thing Vaya exists to say out loud.

**Philippines, home care.** The alternative, and the better product: full-time live-in care in your own condo rather than a facility. Per `research/vaya-care.md`, a licensed private nurse live-in runs about US$1,300 to 1,650 a month at Vaya's sell price. A separate source puts live-in licensed nursing at US$400 to 700 a month ([RetireFinder](https://retirefinder.com/care-abroad/assisted-living-abroad/)). **These do not agree and the gap is the whole margin.** Resolve it with real Placewell quotes before anything is published.

## 4. Why the Philippines wins this specific argument

1. **English.** A dementia patient cannot learn Thai or Spanish. The caregiver has to speak the patient's language, and in the Philippines that is native, not a service upgrade.
2. **Nursing supply.** The Philippines is the world's largest exporter of nurses. The training pipeline exists because the export market built it.
3. **Visa.** 30 days visa-free extendable to 36 months, SRRV from US$1,500 courtesy or US$15,000 with a pension, age floor cut to 40 in September 2025. Cheapest and easiest in the region. See `research/manila-demand.md`.
4. **One-to-one, not 8-to-1.** The Slate family's whole point: US facilities run roughly 8:1 and it is not enough for dementia. Manila's price buys a person, not a slot.
5. **The family can visit.** Direct flights, no language barrier for the visiting adult child, and a Fil-Am family may already have people there.

**Where it loses:** no memory-care infrastructure to speak of, no quality seal (DSWD's Standards Bureau accredits social-welfare facilities, not private for-profit homes), hospital transfer arrangements are on the family to verify, typhoons, and Manila's traffic makes an emergency hospital run genuinely slower. Say all of it.

## 5. The Philippine legal frame, which is not optional

Two laws govern this and getting them wrong ends the business.

- **RA 10361, the Kasambahay Law** (2013). Covers domestic workers. Statutory minimum wage plus mandatory SSS, PhilHealth and Pag-IBIG contributions of roughly PHP 1,460 to 1,710 a month. A live-in helper is an employee with rights, not a service ([Labor Law PH](https://laborlaw.ph/guide-batas-kasambahay-or-domestic-workers-act/), [Alburo Law](https://www.alburolaw.com/minimum-requirements-and-legal-implications-of-hiring-a-kasambahay-or-domestic-worker/)).
- **RA 11965, the Caregiver Welfare Act.** Signed 23 November 2023; IRR issued as DOLE Department Order No. 254 s. 2025 on 21 May 2025 and now in full force. **A worker without TESDA NC II certification, a health degree, or a passed TESDA assessment does not legally qualify as a "caregiver"**, whatever a family or agency calls them. It applies to caregivers in private homes as well as facilities, directly hired or agency-placed ([MaidProvider](https://www.maidprovider.ph/humanplus/kasambahay-law-vs-caregiver-welfare-act)).

**What this means for Vaya.** Selling "a caregiver" to a foreign family while placing an uncertified helper is a labor violation with the customer's name on it. It also means the compliant version is a genuine moat: Placewell already screens, certifies and employs to standard, and a family in Ohio cannot do that from Ohio. **Compliance is the product, not the overhead.**

**Two more constraints:**
- **24/7 coverage needs two staff.** One person cannot legally or humanly work around the clock. Every price quoted for round-the-clock care must be a two-person price. This roughly doubles the number and it is the single most common way this business is mis-sold.
- **Insurance covers none of it.** PhilHealth and most expat health policies explicitly exclude long-term custodial care. SRRV holders pay PHP 15,000 a year into PhilHealth and get no residential care benefit. Care is funded from capital or from an indemnity LTC policy. Nothing else.

## 6. Who is already doing this

Nobody, in the Philippines, in English, as a business.

- **Mexico owns the trend.** Chapala, Lake Chapala, and the Parriva/Slate coverage have made it a recognised option for Americans ([Parriva](https://www.parriva.com/americans-moving-to-mexico-for-elder-care-2026/)).
- **Thailand owns the facilities.** Baan Kamlangchay and Vivobene are twenty-year-old European-run institutions with international press.
- **The Philippines has the cheapest, best-matched supply in the world and no front door.** Search results return blog posts, no operator brand, no named placement company, and facilities that will not publish a price.

That is the opportunity and it is also the warning: it may be empty because it is hard, not because nobody thought of it. The hard parts are liability, medical escalation, and the fact that the customer is a distressed adult child in another timezone.

## 7. The series

The audience is not the patient. **It is the 45-to-60 year old adult child holding the bill.** They are online, they are searching at 2am, and they are the ones who will move.

### Series name, three versions
- **Safe: "The Cost of Care."** Descriptive, searchable, no cleverness. Ranks.
- **Bold: "What Your Money Buys."** Puts the arbitrage in the title. Works across care, housing and staff, so it can outlive this one subject.
- **Weird: "Eight to One."** The US staffing ratio versus one-to-one. Means nothing until you watch, means everything after. Great for one film, weak as a series name.

Invoices, receipts, a policy document, a bank app, a hospital corridor, a face. Money content is the easiest thing in the world to cut at pace because every number is a cut.

## 8. The business behind it

| Layer | Product | Price scaffold | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Free** | Policy check call, 20 minutes | Free | Lead generation, highest intent |
| **Entry** | Care Feasibility Report: their policy, their budget, their parent's condition, whether Manila works | US$250 to 500 | High. It is a call plus a document. |
| **Core** | Placement: nurse or caregiver sourced, certified, employed through Placewell | Placement fee one month's salary, then PHP 5,000 to 8,000/mo management | Recurring, per `research/vaya-care.md` |
| **Wrapper** | The move itself: visa, condo, bank, SIM, driver, Vaya Line | Existing Vaya services | Already built |
| **Recurring** | Family reporting: a monthly check-in call and written update to the adult child in the US | US$150 to 300/mo | Pure margin, and it is what the customer is actually buying |

**The last line is the real business.** The adult child is not buying a nurse. They are buying the ability to stop worrying from 8,000 miles away. Someone who calls them once a month and says "your mother is fine, here is what happened, here is the photo" is worth more than the placement fee and never churns while the parent lives.

**Why this beats every other Vaya line:** contract length. A food tour is one Saturday. A visa filing is annual. Care lasts years, is paid monthly, and the customer's switching cost is enormous because the relationship is with a person their parent trusts.

## 9. The top three ways this goes wrong

1. **A patient is harmed.** The worst case is not a bad review. Vaya must never be the clinical decision-maker: the licensed agency employs, a Philippine physician directs care, and Vaya's role is stated in writing as placement and coordination only. Professional indemnity cover, not just general liability, before the first placement.
2. **It reads as trafficking in cheap labor.** One clipped 30 seconds of a price comparison with no nurse on screen and the whole brand is gone. The mitigation is structural, not editorial: the nurse's pay is on screen, the employer is licensed, episode 5 exists, and `standards.html` already commits Vaya to it publicly.
3. **A family moves a parent who was too frail to move, on Vaya's encouragement.** Every piece of content needs the counter-case in it. There must be a documented "do not do this if" list, and the Feasibility Report has to be able to return "no."

A fourth, smaller: quoting one-person prices for 24/7 care. Legally impossible and it will blow up at month two. Two-person pricing, always.
