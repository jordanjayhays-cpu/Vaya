# How media companies make content people watch, and how they stay alive

Research brief for Vaya. Date: 2026-09-06. Sources inline. UNSURE = could not verify from a primary or reliable secondary source.

Why this exists: Vaya is revenue-first and one city at a time. This is the list of what Vox, Vice, and the newer small-team media companies actually do, what it earns them, and which parts Vaya should copy or avoid.

---

## Part 1. What makes their content engaging

### Vox (explainer video)
- The format is "concept inside its messy reality": one question in the title, then context, history, archive footage, charts, and a tightly scripted narration. Watch time went from about 1 minute to 4+ minutes per view once they committed to 6 to 10 minute explainers ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/future-of-tv/vox-com-tripled-youtube-viewership-one-year/)).
- Video team is its own newsroom, not a repurposing unit. About 60% of videos came from the video team's own reporting; team went 12 to 20 people; cadence about 5 YouTube videos a week ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/future-of-tv/vox-com-tripled-youtube-viewership-one-year/)).
- Series over one-offs. One viral one-off ("Syria's war", 110M+ views) was turned into a recurring series, *Borders*, with a named host (Johnny Harris). Other named series: *Vox Pop*, *Strikethrough*. Channel now about 12.7M subs ([HypeAuditor](https://hypeauditor.com/youtube/UCLXo7UDZvByw2ixzpQCufnA/)).
- Weakness of brand-led: the hosts became the brand and left. Harris, Cleo Abram and others now run bigger independent channels than Vox's video output ([Project C](https://projectc.substack.com/p/more-former-vox-video-journalists)).

### Vice (immersive first-person docs)
- "Immersionism": reporter lives with subjects, local stringers, story emerges in the edit, first-person narration ([Deadline](https://deadline.com/2013/01/shane-smith-calls-vice-style-of-journalism-immersionism-tca-397111/)). Raw aesthetic read as honest to audiences bored by polished TV ([CJR](https://www.cjr.org/analysis/the_cult_of_vice.php)).
- What worked: host-as-guide, going where others wouldn't, verticals with distinct tones (Munchies, Motherboard, Noisey). Munchies was pitched as the anti-studio cooking show ([Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/presenting-munchies-a-new-channel-by-vice/)).
- What failed: copying the format onto linear TV (Viceland), which "never took off" ([Forbes AU](https://www.forbes.com.au/news/investing/inside-the-dramatic-rise-and-fall-of-vice-media/)), and letting brand deals dilute the mission. Colin and Samir's diagnosis: "good creative doesn't scale" at VC multiples, and when about 85% of output is sponsored "you've lost the plot" ([Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/12/13/2024/mixed-signals-how-youtube-became-tv-with-colin-and-samir)).
- Today: Vice News soft-relaunched as hosted video segments funded by brand partnerships ([THR](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/business/business-news/vice-news-returns-shane-smith-1236587574/)); magazine back at 4 issues a year with a subscription ([Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/media/vice-subscription-magazine-relaunch/)).

### Morning Brew
- One daily email, one voice, one promise: "Become smarter in just 5 minutes" ([News Machines](https://newsmachines.substack.com/p/how-morning-brew-uses-data-to-grow)).
- Referral engine with merch tiers (3 referrals = stickers, 25 = hoodie, 100 = VIP event). Referrals drove about 30% of new subs monthly, up to 75% at peak ([ReferralRock](https://referralrock.com/blog/morning-brew-referral-program/)).
- Clone the format into niches: 8 B2B verticals (Marketing Brew, HR Brew, CFO Brew) ([News Machines](https://newsmachines.substack.com/p/how-morning-brew-uses-data-to-grow)).
- Host-led daily video/podcast (*Morning Brew Daily*) hit $5M a year in revenue in under two years ([News Machines](https://newsmachines.substack.com/p/how-morning-brew-uses-data-to-grow)).

### Puck
- Writer = brand. About 11 star reporters each with a personality-driven "private email" newsletter; ads modeled on print magazine ads ([Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/media/puck/)).
- Writers get equity plus $10K per 1,000 subscribers they bring; 2025 added retention bonuses ([Growth Curve](https://www.growthcurvenewsletter.com/p/inside-puck-s-bet-on-writer-ownership)).
- Small paid summits: 100 to 125 people, $1,500 to 2,000 tickets ([Growth Curve](https://www.growthcurvenewsletter.com/p/inside-puck-s-bet-on-writer-ownership)).

### Semafor
- Rigid article template ("Semaform"): facts, then reporter's view, then disagreement, then global views. For breaking news, publish facts first, fill the rest later ([Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/10/18/2022/what-is-a-semaform-anyway-and-why-should-you-care)).
- Newsletter-first, not site-first: 1M+ email subs, sponsorships instead of banners ([Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/01/07/2026/semafor-marks-record-growth-and-profitability-for-global-news-media-launches-major-editorial-and-live-journalism-expansion); [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/semafor-success-comes-less-advertising--more-events)).
- Events are editorial product: 26+ events in 2025; flagship growing from 3 to 5 days ([Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/01/07/2026/semafor-marks-record-growth-and-profitability-for-global-news-media-launches-major-editorial-and-live-journalism-expansion)).

### 404 Media and Defector (worker-owned)
- 404: scoops only, FOIA-heavy, one podcast with subscriber bonus episodes. Growth is word of mouth; they call it their "most important discovery mechanism" ([404 Media](https://www.404media.co/two-years-404-media-anniversary/)).
- Defector: 27 people, zero turnover in 5 years, about 150 freelancers. Best acquisition tactic was a "$5 for 3 months" promo (3,000 trials). Their own words: new-subscriber acquisition "underperformed" and distribution is hard ([Defector](https://defector.com/defector-annual-report-year-five)). Their hit podcast *Normal Gossip* was licensed to Radiotopia while the hosts kept the IP ([Podnews](https://podnews.net/press-release/normal-gossip-radiotopia)).

### Creator-led (Johnny Harris, Yes Theory, Colin and Samir, Nebula)
- Harris: 6.6M subs, 30+ staff, about $100K a month overhead. His thesis: "people want to follow people" and trust now attaches to a person, not an institution ([Tara Palmeri](https://www.tarapalmeri.com/p/youtube-star-johnny-harris-on-his)).
- Yes Theory: travel/discomfort format; monetized via sponsors plus their own apparel brand Seek Discomfort ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/05/06/yes-theory-fan-of-a-fan-acquisition-sale-selery/amp/)).
- Colin and Samir: 32% of their views are on connected TVs, average session about 40 minutes; YouTube rewards niche specificity and titles/thumbnails built for suggested-video traffic ([Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/12/13/2024/mixed-signals-how-youtube-became-tv-with-colin-and-samir)).
- Nebula: $5 a month, 680K+ paying, 50/50 revenue split with creators by watch time ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91037360/nebula-most-innovative-companies-2024)).

### Bloomberg Originals and Eater
- Bloomberg: host-led cinematic series (*Hello World* with Ashlee Vance, *Giant Leap*, *CityLab*), now carried as a linear channel on YouTube TV since Oct 2025 ([Variety](https://variety.com/2025/tv/news/youtube-tv-adds-bloomberg-media-news-originals-channels-1236561221/)).
- Eater: its moat was the city network and maps. That network was cut by roughly a third of union jobs in Aug 2025; pivot to an app, "social-first storytelling, tentpole brand moments" and the trade newsletter *Pre Shift* ([WBEZ](https://www.wbez.org/food-drink/2025/08/12/eater-layoffs-15-staffers-chicago-local-journalism); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/408073/vox-media-lays-off-15-eater-employees.html)).

## Part 2. How they stay afloat (or didn't)

### Vice, the cautionary case
- Peak: $5.7B valuation after TPG's $450M in 2017 ([Variety](https://variety.com/2017/digital/global/vice-cash-tpg-450-million-1202470168/)); Disney put in 2 x $200M in 2015 ([Forbes AU](https://www.forbes.com.au/news/investing/inside-the-dramatic-rise-and-fall-of-vice-media/)).
- 2022: projected $700M revenue, landed about $600M, below 2021's $680M ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/vice-media-2022-revenue-target-miss/)). Unprofitable overall.
- May 2023: Chapter 11 with about $834M in debt obligations ([Forbes AU](https://www.forbes.com.au/news/investing/inside-the-dramatic-rise-and-fall-of-vice-media/)); sold to lenders Fortress, Soros and Monroe for $350M, wiping out TPG and James Murdoch ([Variety](https://variety.com/2023/digital/news/vice-media-closes-sale-post-bankruptcy-investors-fortress-soros-monroe-1235683295/); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/05/15/vice-media-files-for-bankruptcy-to-enable-sale-to-lenders-including-soros-and-fortress.html)).
- Feb 2024: vice.com shut, several hundred laid off, shift to a "studio model" ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/02/23/vice-media-shutting-vice-com-laying-off-several-hundred-staff)). May 2024: digital brands handed to a JV with Savage Ventures; Vice kept Vice Studios, Vice TV (with A+E) and ad agency Virtue ([Variety](https://variety.com/2024/digital/news/vice-digital-joint-venture-savage-ventures-vice-news-1235997565/)).
- Cause: ad-dependent revenue while Google and Meta took the spend, a cost base built for $700M, a failed TV bet, and debt.
- Lesson: never let headcount and fixed cost run ahead of revenue you can see, and don't take money that requires you to become a TV network.

### Vox Media, diversified, then dismantled
- Revenue mix: ads (own sites plus Concert, a marketplace selling across NBCU, Conde Nast, Hearst and BuzzFeed inventory with Vox first-party data ([Concert](https://concert.io/), [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/performance-marketing/vox-media-creates-a-digital-ad-network-for-local-publications/))), podcasts (30+ shows, 10M+ monthly listeners), NYMag subscriptions, Vox membership ($5 a month, launched May 2024 after 100K+ donations ([CNN](https://edition.cnn.com/2024/05/20/media/vox-relaunch-reliable-sources/index.html))), Eater/Thrillist/Punch affiliate and licensing, Vox Media Studios. UNSURE on exact company revenue; secondary sources cite about $600M in 2022 falling about 15% in 2023.
- Layoffs: 7% (about 130) Jan 2023 ([Variety](https://variety.com/2023/digital/news/vox-media-layoffs-130-employees-job-cuts-1235496467/)), 4% Nov 2023, lifestyle brands Dec 2024, Polygon sold May 2025, Eater cut Aug 2025.
- 2026 endgame: James Murdoch's Lupa Systems bought NYMag, Vox.com and the podcast network (reported about $300M; terms undisclosed; closed July 8, 2026 ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/05/20/vox-media-lupa-systems-james-murdoch), [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/lupa-systems-completes-acquisition-of-new-york-magazine-vox-media-podcast-network-and-vox-302821001.html))). Penske bought the rest (Eater, The Verge, SB Nation, Thrillist, Punch, Vox Studios) on June 18, 2026 ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/06/22/penske-vox-media); [Variety](https://variety.com/2026/biz/news/pmc-vox-media-ryan-pauley-verge-eater-1236784682/)).
- Lesson: diversification worked on paper, but the assets that held value were podcasts and a subscription magazine. Ad-funded lifestyle sites (Eater, Thrillist) were the discards.

### Morning Brew
- Sold 75% to Insider for $75M in Oct 2020 on about $20M revenue; Axel Springer bought the rest Feb 2025 ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2020/10/29/insider-inc-buys-majority-stake-morning-brew); [Talking Biz News](https://talkingbiznews.com/media-news/axel-springer-buys-remaining-stake-in-morning-brew/)).
- About $70M revenue on track for 2025; B2B newsletters $25M in 2024; multimedia over $10M ([News Machines](https://newsmachines.substack.com/p/how-morning-brew-uses-data-to-grow)). Mix: newsletter sponsorships, B2B, podcast/video ads, events.
- Lesson: free product, paid by sponsors, growth by referral, then sell into a strategic. Bootstrapped to a sale in 5 years.

### The Athletic
- Bought by NYT in 2022 for $550M; lost money until Q3 2024 (first profit $2.6M ([Awful Announcing](https://awfulannouncing.com/athletic/now-profitable-third-quarter-2024.html))). Q4 2024 revenue $49.7M (+29%), Q1 2025 ad revenue +82.5% to $10.4M ([SEC 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/71691/000007169125000021/pressrelease12312024.htm); [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/05/20/nyt-athletic-profitable)).
- Got there by cutting 4% of the newsroom and abandoning one-reporter-per-team local coverage ([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/commentary/2023/athletic-lays-off-20-stops-beat-coverage-each-team-nhl-mlb/)), cutting local podcasts ([Nieman](https://www.niemanlab.org/2023/10/the-athletic-cuts-back-on-local-podcasts/)), and bundling.
- Lesson: local-by-local subscription coverage only turned profitable inside a bundle with a parent that could sell ads. Standalone, it burned cash for 6 years.

### Puck
- About $20M revenue in 2025 (+40%); ads and sponsorship are the majority, subs +50% ([Growth Curve](https://www.growthcurvenewsletter.com/p/inside-puck-s-bet-on-writer-ownership)). About 100K paying subs including Air Mail (bought Oct 2025 for about $16M, mostly stock). Series B valued it about $70M. "Very close" to profitable, not there ([A Media Operator](https://www.amediaoperator.com/analysis/what-the-puck/)).
- Lesson: a "subscription" brand that actually lives on premium sponsorships. Talent equity keeps stars from leaving.

### Semafor
- $40M revenue in 2025, $2M EBITDA, first profitable year; roughly half ads, half events; $30M raised at a $330M valuation in Jan 2026 ([Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/01/07/2026/semafor-marks-record-growth-and-profitability-for-global-news-media-launches-major-editorial-and-live-journalism-expansion); [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/semafor-success-comes-less-advertising--more-events); [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/01/07/semafor-raises-30-million)).
- Lesson: events aren't a side business. They're half the P&L.

### 404 Media and Defector
- 404: profitable within 6 months ([Nieman](https://www.niemanlab.org/2024/02/six-months-in-journalist-owned-tech-publication-404-media-is-profitable/)); $100 a year subs; ads, podcast, merch, licensing to WIRED, events. No public numbers.
- Defector year 5: $4.65M revenue ($3.8M subs, $850K other) against $4.55M costs; flat year over year; about $100K margin ([Defector](https://defector.com/defector-annual-report-year-five)).
- Lesson: worker-owned subs pay salaries. They don't grow. Defector is plateaued at about $4.6M.

### Creators
- Colin and Samir: about 90% of revenue from sponsorships and events, about 10% AdSense ([Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/12/13/2024/mixed-signals-how-youtube-became-tv-with-colin-and-samir)). Harris: AdSense "millions" a year against $1.2M a year overhead ([Tara Palmeri](https://www.tarapalmeri.com/p/youtube-star-johnny-harris-on-his)). Yes Theory sold its merch operation (Fan of a Fan) in 2025 ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/05/06/yes-theory-fan-of-a-fan-acquisition-sale-selery/amp/)).
- Bloomberg Media: 707K paying subs, revenue +6%, subs +10% in 2025 ([Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/media/bloomberg-media-700000-subscribers-2025/)). Video is a marketing cost for a terminal business, not a P&L.

### The closest analog: Time Out
- FY2025: Time Out Market (food halls) revenue +9% to £46.7M and profitable; Time Out Media revenue -26% to £26.6M and loss-making, blamed on social, AI search and the US ad slowdown ([Halifax/Sharecast](https://www.investments.halifax.co.uk/research-centre/news-centre/article/?id=21398438&type=bsm)). 10 markets open, 6 more planned by FY27 ([Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/company-news/time-out-group-reports-dip-in-revenue-market-growth-93CH-3882629)).
- Lesson: a city-media brand's real-world business outgrew and now subsidizes its media. That is Vaya's thesis, proven at scale.

## Part 3. Takeaways for Vaya (revenue-first, one city at a time)

1. **Experiences are the business; media is the funnel.** Time Out's food halls carry its media. Semafor is 50% events. Puck sells $2K summit tickets. Price the food tours and concierge first, build video to fill them.
2. **Host-led, not brand-led.** Trust attaches to a person now (Harris). Vox lost its hosts and its edge. Make the Manila host the face, and give them equity like Puck does so they don't walk.
3. **One repeatable format with a named series.** Vox's *Borders*, Bloomberg's *Hello World*. Pick 1 or 2 series ("Moving to Manila: X, explained", "Eat here, not there") and run them weekly. No one-offs until the series works.
4. **Steal the Semaform for city explainers.** Facts / our view / where locals disagree / what expats get wrong. It's fast to write and reads honest.
5. **Email is the asset, YouTube is the reach.** Everyone profitable here (Morning Brew, Semafor, Puck, 404) owns a list. Put the Morning Brew referral ladder on the newsletter from day one; reward with tour discounts, not stickers.
6. **Sponsors over subscriptions for a small audience.** Colin and Samir 90/10, Puck majority ads, Morning Brew all sponsor. Sell Manila property developers, banks, co-working, relocation firms as newsletter and series sponsors. Don't paywall before about 10K engaged readers.
7. **But cap the sponsored share of output.** The Vice and BuzzFeed failure mode: when most content is a brand deal, viewers leave. Keep it under about 25% of episodes.
8. **Build for TV-length, not TikTok-length.** A third of creator views are on TVs; average sessions about 40 minutes. 8 to 15 minute city docs age well and get suggested for years. Shorts are trailers, not product.
9. **Fixed cost is the killer.** Vice built for $700M and got $600M. Defector at 27 people is flat at $4.6M. Stay under 5 FTE per city; use freelancers (Defector uses about 150).
10. **Do not copy:** Vice's TV/platform expansion; a Vox-style portfolio of ad-funded verticals; The Athletic's reporter-per-beat before proving demand; Eater's SEO-map model (Google Zero killed it); raising VC that needs a 10x media exit (Puck has been "very close to profitable" for 5 years).
11. **Don't expand cities until city one has three revenue lines each paying for itself** (tours, sponsors, concierge). The Athletic and Eater both learned that a 20-city network is 20 separate P&Ls, most of them negative.
12. **Have an exit in mind that isn't an IPO.** Morning Brew sold to a strategic at about 4x revenue. A profitable city-media-plus-experiences brand is a buyable asset for a Time Out, a travel company, or a property group. Vice's $5.7B was never real.
