# FDI projects to film, and the workflow from video to Vaya Desk

Date: 2026-09-13. Companion to `research/fdi-philippines.md`, which sets the legal frame: Vaya sells information and access on flat fees, never capital, never a percentage. This document is the inventory of what to film and the funnel that turns a viewer into a paying Desk client. Sources inline. All prices are scaffolds.

---

## Part 1: The workflow

### The shape, in one line

**Film the situation, not the service. Land the viewer on a page built for that one episode. Qualify them, including the power to say no. Sell a flat-fee report. Hand delivery to a licensed partner.**

### The five steps

| Step | What happens | Why it is built this way |
|---|---|---|
| **1. The film** | A real situation with real numbers. An empty tower, a landlord's asking price, a payroll spreadsheet. Vaya Desk is one line at the end, under 15 seconds. | Same segue rule as every other Vaya line (`research/visitor-mvps.md`). The film that sells is the one that was not trying to. |
| **2. The episode page** | Not a generic contact form. `vayanews.com/desk/ep14` with the episode's own numbers, the ownership rule that applies, and one form. | A viewer who watched a film about a specific thing and lands on a generic page leaves. The page continues the film. |
| **3. The free thing** | A one-page data sheet: the vacancy figure, the rent band, the capital threshold, the three IRS forms. Email required. | This is the list. Every outlet studied built the list before the revenue (`research/media-playbook.md`). |
| **4. The qualifier** | Six questions. Budget band, timeline, sector, US person or not, whether they have visited, what they have already been told. **It must be able to return "not yet."** | The fastest way to kill this business is to sell a briefing to someone who was never going to invest. A refund is worse than a lost sale. |
| **5. The paid thing** | The Brief, or The Ground Check. Flat fee, invoiced and paid **before** the outcome is known. Delivery by a licensed partner where a licence is needed. | Flat and prepaid is the compliance position, not just the pricing. See `fdi-philippines.md` section 3. |

### The rules that never bend

- **Attribution is by episode code.** EP14 on the page, on the invoice, in the spreadsheet. Revenue per film is the only success metric that counts.
- **Vaya never signs, holds, routes, or takes a share.** Stated on the page, every page.
- **Every rule cited carries the date it was checked.** These rules moved three times between 2021 and 2022.
- **Disclosure applies to money exactly as it applies to restaurants.** If a partner delivers the work and Vaya is paid, it is said on camera.

## Part 2: Six funnel scenarios

Different buyers need different funnels. One film cannot serve a retiree buying a condo and a US company opening a back office.

### Scenario 1: The Distressed Unit
**Buyer:** Fil-Am or retiree, US$80,000 to 250,000, decides in weeks.
**Film:** "There are 79,000 unsold condos in Metro Manila. Here is what that does to the price you should pay." Walk three buildings. Show three asking prices and three real vacancy rates.
**Click:** "Check the building you are looking at."
**Free:** vacancy and rent band for the top twenty Metro Manila buildings, dated.
**Paid:** **The Ground Check**, US$1,500. Someone physically goes, photographs the unit, the lobby at 9pm, the parking, the water pressure, the neighbours, and writes it up.
**Partner:** none needed. This is journalism with an invoice.
**Volume:** highest of any scenario. This is the workhorse.

### Scenario 2: The Back Office
**Buyer:** US small-business owner, 5 to 30 people, US$300,000 to 1M over three years, decides in months.
**Film:** "What a fifteen-person team in Manila actually costs." Real payroll, real office rent, real electricity, real internet, the 13th month pay nobody budgets for.
**Click:** "Get the cost model for your headcount."
**Free:** a one-page cost-per-seat calculator.
**Paid:** **The Brief**, US$2,000. Their sector's ownership rule, which capital threshold applies, whether the fifteen-Filipino-employee exemption gets them from US$200,000 to US$100,000, BOI versus PEZA, and the US filings they will owe.
**Partner:** **Placewell delivers the hiring.** This is the scenario where Vaya's content feeds an existing business with real capacity.
**Note:** the exemption is a hiring plan. That is the whole insight.

### Scenario 3: The Ghost Floor
**Buyer:** opportunistic, developer, or nobody. This one is for reach.
**Film:** "I went inside an empty POGO tower." Metro Manila office vacancy has sat around 19 to 19.4% since late 2025, the highest in over a decade, with roughly 260,000 sqm vacated directly by offshore gaming operators and Bay Area residential vacancy at 57.3% ([Colliers](https://www.colliers.com/en-ph/news/rethinking-office-market-occupiers-developers-should-know-pogo-ban), [InsiderPH](https://insiderph.com/offshore-gaming-exit-reshapes-metro-manila-office-market-opens-doors-for-expansion)).
**Click:** nothing, at first.
**Why:** this is the film that travels. It earns the audience the other five monetise. Put the Desk link in the description and do not mention it on camera.
**Follow-up:** a second film, three months later, on what the landlords did with the space. That one sells.

### Scenario 4: The Operator
**Buyer:** Fil-Am who wants to put money into the country their family is from, US$25,000 to 150,000.
**Film:** a Filipino operator with a working business and no capital. Their numbers on screen. Not a pitch, a portrait.
**Click:** "Talk to us about how this works."
**Paid:** **a flat monthly retainer for introductions and coordination.** Never a share of the deal. Never a success fee. This is the scenario with the highest legal risk and the fee structure is the entire mitigation.
**Warning:** this is one step from Puhunan and from a broker-dealer problem. Do not run it before the US securities lawyer has answered.

### Scenario 5: The Care Facility
**Buyer:** a US investor who watched Glocal and did the arithmetic.
**Film:** the Glocal care series itself. No new film needed.
**The insight:** **Glocal creates the demand and the FDI line sells the supply.** A series showing Americans that Manila care costs a fifth of US care creates, in one subset of viewers, the thought "somebody should build more of these." Elder care is not on the negative list, it is labour-intensive so the fifteen-employee exemption is trivially met, and Placewell staffs it.
**Paid:** The Brief, then Placewell for the workforce.
**This is the flywheel.** Two strands, one customer base, and Vaya is paid at both ends without touching a peso of anyone's capital.

### Scenario 6: The Big Bet
**Buyer:** institutional. You will not get them and should not chase them.
**Film:** *The Read*, the existing quarterly contrarian series. Renewables took PHP 343.47 billion in the first half of 2026, 74.25% of all approved investment ([Manila Bulletin](https://mb.com.ph/2026/08/24/renewables-data-centers-thriving-fdi-big-bets-for-philippines)); HSBC calls renewables and AI data centres the sweet spots.
**Click:** the newsletter.
**Why bother:** credibility. Being visibly right about the big picture is what makes a US$1,500 Ground Check feel cheap. Do not build a product on it.

## The top three ways this goes wrong

1. **A film reads as a recommendation to buy.** "This building is a good deal" plus a link to a partner is investment advice with an undisclosed interest. Films describe situations and name what is uncertain. The Ground Check reports what is there; it does not conclude "buy."
2. **Someone acts on a rule that has changed and loses money.** Every rule on screen and on every page carries the date checked and the citation. A rule older than twelve months gets rechecked or pulled.
3. **The Operator scenario drifts into a success fee.** It is the most natural drift in the whole business and it is the one that ends it. Flat retainers only, invoiced before the outcome is known, and not at all until the US securities lawyer has answered.

## Accept when

Every scenario has one film, one episode page, one free sheet, one qualifier that can say no, and one flat-fee product, with an episode code running from the film to the invoice. And the first Ground Check has been sold, delivered and filmed.
