## Payments for foreigners: what actually works (checked 2026-09-06)

This is the detail that breaks Vaya Landing if we get it wrong, and it is the reason the service is worth paying for.

- **Maya, not GCash, is the foreigner's wallet.** GCash KYC is built around Philippine government IDs, which for a foreigner means an ACR I-Card, SRRV ID, Alien Employment Permit or Philippine driver's licence ([liveinph](https://liveinph.com/gcash-foreigner-philippines-account-limits-2026), [pinoyloads](https://pinoyloads.com/gcash-for-foreigners)). Maya's Consumer tier verifies on a passport alone and handles US, UK, EU, Australian and Canadian passports ([pinoyloads](https://pinoyloads.com/maya-for-foreigners-verification-passport-dead-sim-trap), [Akinix](https://akinix.com/en/digital-banking-in-the-philippines-for-foreigners-maya-gcash-and-alternatives/)).
- **GCash has a tourist option, with limits.** "GTourist" is a 30-day wallet, passport only, PHP 50,000 cap, and it is built around a US (+1) mobile number ([Bel Around The World](https://www.belaroundtheworld.com/cashless-in-the-philippines/)). Fine for a two-week visitor, useless for a month-stayer.
- **The SIM trap, and the reason Landing is a real product.** Under the SIM Registration Act a tourist SIM expires in about 30 days, and prolonged prepaid inactivity kills it too. A dead SIM breaks the SMS one-time-password chain that every login, device swap and password reset depends on, which locks the wallet ([pinoyloads](https://pinoyloads.com/maya-for-foreigners-verification-passport-dead-sim-trap)). **Registering the SIM properly and warning the guest about expiry is part of Vaya Landing.** Nobody else does this and it is the single most common way a visitor's first week goes wrong.
- **The unlock is the visa extension.** The ACR I-Card that comes with going past 59 days is what opens full GCash and most bank accounts. That is the Visa Desk feeding the payments problem, and it is worth saying on camera.

## Travel eSIM versus local eSIM: why Landing still exists (checked 2026-09-13)

The obvious objection to Landing is "just use Airalo or Holafly." Answer it honestly on camera, because it is right for some people.

- **Airalo and Holafly are data only. No phone number, no SMS, so no OTP.** Neither sells a Philippine plan with a number attached; you get internet and use WhatsApp for calls ([esimDB](https://esimdb.com/philippines/airalo), [Freaking Nomads](https://freakingnomads.com/best-esims-for-philippines/)). For a two-week visitor who needs maps and messaging, **that is genuinely the easier answer and Vaya should say so.**
- **They cannot do the one thing that matters here.** Maya, GCash, Grab and every delivery app verify by SMS to a **+63** number. No local number, no wallet, no Grab.
- **The fix is a native Globe or Smart eSIM**, which provisions a real +63 number ([pinoyloads](https://pinoyloads.com/globe-esim-philippines), [ExpatDen](https://www.expatden.com/philippines/sim-registration-philippines-foreigners/)). **This replaces the physical SIM in the Landing spec:** no plastic, no carrier-store queue, and it can be provisioned before the guest even lands.
- **Registration still applies.** RA 11934 covers the subscription, not the card, so a local eSIM needs the same documents: passport, proof of local address (a hotel or Airbnb booking works), and a return or onward ticket ([globesimsreg](https://globesimsreg.com/registration-for-tourists/), [Smart](https://smartsimreg.ph/smart-esim-registration/)). **Collecting those three things in advance is the work Landing is actually selling.**
- **And here is the cross-sell.** A tourist SIM runs on a **30-day clock and deactivates automatically at the end of it unless you show an approved visa extension.** Dead SIM means no OTP, which means a locked wallet. **The visa extension is what keeps the phone alive. Landing and Visa Desk are the same service sold twice**, and that is worth saying on camera.

**So the honest line in the film:** if you are here two weeks, buy an Airalo, it is easier. If you are here a month or want to pay for anything with your phone, you need a +63 number, and getting one is more annoying than it should be.

## How the segue works, every time

- The video shows the problem happening to a real person. No "in this video we'll show you our service."
- The service is one line at the end and a link in the description. Under 15 seconds of screen time.
- The person delivering the service appears in the video doing the thing (the driver drives, the guide guides). Trust attaches to them.
- The newsletter carries the same pairing the day the video posts.
