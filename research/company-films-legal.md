# The legal side of a paid company film

Date: 2026-09-13. What has to be true, signed and said before Vaya publishes a film a company paid for. Sources inline. **Not legal advice.** UNSURE = not verified.

---

## 1. Start here: this conflicts with your own standards page

`standards.html` currently says, in public:

> **"Nobody pays to be recommended.** Our partners are paid to deliver a service. **No business can buy a place in a film**, on a tour route, or on this site."

> **"We take no sponsors.** Vaya makes money when someone books something. There is no advertiser whose opinion we have to manage."

**A company paying Vaya to make a film about it is a business buying a place in a film.** As written, the MVP breaks the promise on the page.

This is not a technicality. That page is the thing that makes everything else Vaya says believable, and it is already published.

### The fix: two labels and one firewall

| | **Vaya editorial** | **Puhunan** |
|---|---|---|
| Who pays | Nobody. Ever | The company |
| What it is | Reviews, guides, rankings, Block by Block, Glocal, Eat Here Not There | One company's story, told honestly, that they commissioned |
| Said on screen | Nothing needed | **"[Company] paid us to make this film."** In the first fifteen seconds |
| Can it recommend? | Yes. That is the point | **No. It is not a recommendation and never presented as one** |

**The firewall rule, which is what keeps the promise true:** *a company that has paid Vaya never appears in an editorial recommendation, ranking or guide.* Not that year, not quietly, not "because they're genuinely good." **Taking their money costs them the recommendation.** Say that to them up front; it is also a useful filter for who wants a film for the right reason.

### Proposed wording change to `standards.html`
Add under the four rules:

> **Some films are commissioned, and we say so.** A company can pay us to tell its story. When that happens we say it on screen in the first fifteen seconds, and that company is then permanently ineligible for any Vaya recommendation, ranking or guide. Paying us buys a film. It does not buy an opinion.

**This is Jordan's call, not Claude's.** Logged as an open decision, not a made one. The public page is not changed until he says so.

## 2. The paperwork stack

Six documents. One Philippine lawyer drafts the set once and every film reuses it. **Estimate, not a quote: US$800 to 2,000 for the template set**, versus paying per film.

| # | Document | Signed by | The clause that matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Production agreement** | The company | Scope, price, payment terms, delivery, and **who has final cut** (see section 3) |
| 2 | **Appearance release** | The founder and **every person on camera**, including staff | Right to use their image, and their right to withdraw before publication. `outreach/05-release-form.md` exists; have the lawyer check it |
| 3 | **Location release** | The property owner | Permission to film the premises. A mall, industrial estate or leased factory usually needs the landlord's permit too |
| 4 | **Videographer copyright assignment** | The videographer | **The one everyone forgets. See section 4** |
| 5 | **Music licence** | N/A | Proof of licence for every second of audio |
| 6 | **Licence back to the company** | Vaya to the company | What they may do with the film, where, and for how long |

## 3. The question that decides the product: who has final cut

This is the fork, and it has to be answered in writing before the first shoot.

**Option A. Vaya keeps the cut.** The company gets a factual accuracy pass and the right to withdraw consent before publication, and that is all. The film is honest and Vaya's name means something.

**Option B. The company approves the final cut.** Then it is their film, Vaya is a production house for hire, and it must be labelled as such.

**Recommendation: A, with an explicit accuracy pass.** It is the only version compatible with the Vaya voice, and the founders worth filming will accept it. **Whichever is chosen, it goes in the production agreement.** Discovering the disagreement in the edit is how these relationships end.

## 4. Under Philippine law, paying for a film does not give you its copyright

**IP Code (RA 8293) section 178.4:** where a work is commissioned by someone who is not the creator's employer and who pays for it, **the commissioner owns the work, but the copyright remains with the creator, unless there is a written stipulation to the contrary** ([NDV Law](https://ndvlaw.com/who-owns-the-copyright-in-a-commissioned-work/), [Manila Times](https://www.manilatimes.net/2025/06/06/legal-advice/ownership-and-infringement-of-copyright-in-commissioned-digital-artworks/2128392)).

**So hiring a Filipino videographer on a handshake means Vaya does not own the copyright in its own films.** They do.

`DECISIONS.md` already records host and videographer deals as "work for hire, Vaya owns the footage." **Under Philippine law that is only true if it is written down and signed.** Fix it with one clause in the videographer's engagement letter, before the first shoot day, and retrofit it for anyone already booked.

## 5. The five real risks

### Risk 1: Philippine FDA health claims. This one is criminal.

Under the FDA Act of 2009, **all health product advertising must be reviewed and approved by the FDA before publication**, and advertising may only carry claims already approved on the product label or by the FDA ([NDV Law](https://ndvlaw.com/prohibited-claims-in-food-product-labels-under-fda-regulations-and-philippine-law/), [FDA Philippines](https://www.fda.gov.ph/draft-for-comments-guidelines-on-the-use-of-acceptable-nutrition-and-health-claims-in-the-labeling-advertisement-sponsorships-promotions-and-other-marketing-activities-of-prepackaged-processed/)).

**Penalties: one to ten years imprisonment, or a fine of PHP 50,000 to 500,000, or both.** For a manufacturer, importer or distributor: five to ten years and PHP 500,000 to 5,000,000.

**Why this is a live risk for exactly these leads.** Half the seed list in `company-film-leads.md` is honey, carabao milk, raw food and fermented products. A founder saying "our honey boosts immunity" or "this is good for your blood pressure" on camera, published by Vaya, is an unapproved health claim in a promotional material.

**The rule, and it is absolute:**
> **No health, medical, nutritional or therapeutic claim appears in any Vaya film. Ever.**
> Taste, origin, process, ingredients, price, the founder's story, what it costs to make: all fine.
> "Good for you," "boosts," "helps with," "natural remedy," "cures": cut it. Cut it in the edit even if they said it.

Brief the founder before the camera rolls, and make it a line in the production agreement so they are not surprised when it is cut.

> UNSURE whether a documentary-style film about a company counts as "advertising" requiring FDA pre-approval, or whether that applies only to conventional ads. **A film the company paid for is much more likely to count.** Ask the lawyer directly.

### Risk 2: US disclosure. The FTC, not just your conscience.

Vaya publishes to Americans, so the FTC Endorsement Guides apply. A material connection must be disclosed **clear and conspicuously**: in the video itself, at the start, not buried in a description ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking)). **Free product counts as a material connection**, not just cash. Reporting through 2025 and 2026 indicates stepped-up enforcement against creators who disclose only at the end or bury it in the description.

**Practically: the on-screen disclosure in section 1 is not just a brand choice, it is the compliance position.** First fifteen seconds, on screen and spoken, plus the first line of the description.

### Risk 3: Music
The most common way a small production gets struck or sued. **Licensed library music only, licence receipt filed with the project.** No "it's only background," no "it's a small channel."

### Risk 4: Libel
Already flagged in `PLAYBOOK.md`. In a company film the risk is naming a **competitor** unfavourably, or repeating the founder's complaint about a supplier or a rival. **Cut competitor names unless the point is factual, sourced and necessary.**

### Risk 5: Data Privacy Act
Filming employees and customers means processing personal data. Employees sign releases. For customers and passers-by, post a visible notice at the location on the shoot day and avoid identifiable faces of anyone who has not consented. **UNSURE on the exact requirements under RA 10173 for incidental filming; ask the lawyer.**
