# Pre-launch checklist: what real media companies put in place first

Date: 2026-09-06. Operational, legal, and distribution mechanics, not strategy. Sources inline. UNSURE = could not verify. Companion to `PLAYBOOK.md`; the items here that became rules are in the playbook, section 10.

## The one dated item

**YouTube's monetization bar roughly doubles for new applicants on 1 February 2027.** Today: 1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000 qualified watch hours in 12 months or 10 million qualified Shorts views in 90 days, no active strikes ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851)). From 1 Feb 2027: 8,000 watch hours in 365 days, or 20 million Shorts views in 90 days. Channels already in the programme are grandfathered ([YouTube blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-partner-program-updates-2027-new-opportunities-earn/)).

Launching in late 2026 leaves about five months to clear the easier bar. That is a real deadline. It is not the reason to launch, but it changes the cost of drifting.

## 1. How they actually launched

| Outlet | The pattern |
|---|---|
| **Defector** | Splash page collected about 7,000 emails before launch. Announced July 2020, podcast August, site September. Day one: 10,000+ paid at $8/month ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/defector-subscriptions/), [Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/sports/2020/07/28/deadspin-defector-staffers/)). |
| **404 Media** | Launched with four original investigations on day one, not one ([Welcome to 404 Media](https://www.404media.co/welcome-to-404-media/)). Free for about a week, then a partial paywall. Founders each put in about $1,000 for domain, email, Ghost. Profitable by month six ([Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2024/02/six-months-in-journalist-owned-tech-publication-404-media-is-profitable/)). |
| **Semafor** | Eight newsletters live on morning one, after 10 months and $25M ([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/commentary/2022/semafor-the-much-anticipated-global-news-startup-finally-launches/)). |
| **Puck** | Landing page August 2021, full launch September 2021 ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2021/08/04/puck-media-startup)). |
| **Axios** | Newsletters launched before the site ([Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2017/01/axios-launches-newsletters-gearing-up-toward-a-full-fledged-site-just-in-time-for-the-trump-administration/)). |
| **The Athletic** | Chicago only, three writers, four teams, then market by market ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2016/07/08/the-athletic-is-bringing-subscription-based-local-sports-coverage-to-a-city-near-you)). The Manila-then-next-city model, proven. |
| **Morning Brew** | First list built by hand, pitching in university classes. Referral programme later drove close to 30% of growth from 100k to 1.5M ([Referral Rock](https://referralrock.com/blog/morning-brew-referral-program/)). |
| **The Information** | Dec 2013, eight staff, $399/year, self-funded under $1M ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/information-subscription-model/)). Subscribers still "low thousands" a year in. |

**What every one of them did:** a landing page collecting emails months before launch, and a cluster of finished work held for day one.

## 2. YouTube mechanics

- Thresholds and the 2027 change: above.
- **Paid promotion is two obligations.** Tick "contains paid promotion" in Studio, which shows a 10-second viewer disclosure. YouTube states this does not discharge the legal duty: "You and the brands you work with are responsible for understanding and complying with local and legal obligations" ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/154235)). Vaya selling its own tour is self-promotion; a restaurant comping a meal is a material connection and must be disclosed.
- **Cadence and packaging.** Paddy Galloway: one video a week for a new channel, and never publish what would not rank in your channel's top 20%. Top creators spend about 30% of their time on ideation and packaging versus about 5% for small creators ([Colin and Samir](https://www.colinandsamir.com/resources/the-new-rules-of-youtube-from-paddy-galloway)). This supports Vaya's two-films-a-month cadence over volume.
- UNSURE, treat as folklore: whether posting a backlog at once helps or hurts, and the "first 24 to 48 hours" claim. No authoritative source found. Same for channel-trailer and playlist best practice.

## 3. Standards infrastructure

The Markup's ethics page is the best small-publisher model ([themarkup.org/ethics](https://themarkup.org/ethics)). Six components worth copying:

1. **Corrections.** Fix quickly, in the place the material appeared, and be transparent about every post-publication update.
2. **Conflicts of interest.** Disclosed in-article; no trading in covered companies.
3. **Pre-publication review firewall.** Nobody outside editorial sees a story before publication.
4. **Travel.** "Always pay our own way when traveling for editorial purposes." This one matters most for Vaya, which will be offered free hotel nights and comped meals in Manila.
5. **Data and methodology.** Publish underlying data, label estimates, state limits.
6. **AI disclosure.** Label any AI-generated text or imagery.

Hell Gate keeps a firewall between coverage decisions and all revenue ([Hell Gate](https://hellgatenyc.com/about/)). Defector publishes freelancer policies made with the National Writers Union ([Defector](https://defector.com/freelancer-policies)), relevant to paying local correspondents.

UNSURE: no dedicated ethics or corrections page found for 404 Media. A standards page is common, not universal.

**The gap no template covers.** None of these outlets sell services to the businesses they cover. Vaya runs food tours and names restaurants. That needs a disclosure line of its own: whether a featured business pays, is commissioned on bookings, or is on the tour route. This is Vaya's sharpest exposure and it has to be written before the first film.

## 4. Legal and risk

**Media liability (errors and omissions).** Covers defamation, libel, privacy, copyright and IP infringement, plagiarism, and defence costs ([Insureon](https://www.insureon.com/media-business-insurance/cost), [Digital Media Law Project](https://www.dmlp.org/legal-guide/media-liability-insurance)). Cost figures come from broker marketing and vary widely: Insureon reports an average around $78 a month for media professionals; other brokers cite $2,500 to $15,000 a year for $1M limits. Indicative only, not quotes.

**General liability for tours.** Brokers put $1M cover at roughly $350 to $700 a year ([Gondola](https://gondola.travel/tour-operator-insurance/)). Viator and TripAdvisor require operators to carry it and may demand certificates. Some jurisdictions require proof before issuing an operating licence.

**Waivers are weaker than founders assume.** A waiver acknowledges inherent risk. It does not cover gross negligence or equipment faults, is often unenforceable for minors, and is void or limited in several jurisdictions ([Atlas](https://atlasperk.com/guides/operations-compliance-for-travel/liability-waivers/)). Not a substitute for insurance, protocols, or training.

**Duty of care** for guided experiences extends to emergency response planning, supervision ratios, and verifying that activity partners (drivers, guides, boat operators) maintain their own safety standards ([WaiverForever](https://blog.waiverforever.com/guide-duty-of-care-liability-adventure-tourism/)). Vaya's driver service and nurse placement both create third-party exposure.

**FTC endorsement guides,** revised 29 June 2023, first update in 14 years ([Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/insights/2023/07/ftc-advertising-endorsement-and-testimonial-guides)):
- Affiliate links are a material connection per se. No exemption for small commissions or small followings.
- Disclosure must be unavoidable and in the same format as the endorsement. Audible endorsement, audible disclosure. A "click for more" link, fine print, or a bio-page disclosure is not enough ([Arnold and Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2023/07/ftc-endorsement-guides)).
- "Affiliate link" alone is considered inadequate wording.
- The FTC sent 700+ Notices of Penalty Offenses in 2021, exposing recipients to civil penalties around $50,000 per violation ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/enforcement/notices-penalty-offenses/penalty-offenses-concerning-endorsements)).

Non-US regulators YouTube names as the creator's responsibility: ASA (UK), DGCCRF (France), Medienanstalten (Germany), KFTC (Korea). UNSURE on Philippine advertising disclosure rules; needs local counsel.

## 5. Newsletter mechanics

Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender rules, in force since February 2024 ([Resend](https://resend.com/blog/gmail-and-yahoo-bulk-sending-requirements-for-2024), [dmarcian](https://dmarcian.com/yahoo-and-google-dmarc-required/)):

- SPF **and** DKIM configured on the sending domain. Both, not either.
- DMARC published, minimum `p=none`.
- One-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058 List-Unsubscribe), honoured within two days.
- Spam complaints under 0.3%. Google recommends under 0.1%; 0.3% is where enforcement starts.
- Sending 5,000+ messages a day to Gmail addresses triggers permanent bulk-sender classification. It does not lapse if volume drops.

Set all of this up before the first send. Authentication failure on a new domain is the standard way a launch newsletter lands in spam.

**Consent.** GDPR does not literally mandate double opt-in but requires unambiguous, affirmative, specific, informed, freely given consent; double opt-in is the practical evidence, and Germany, Austria and Norway effectively require it ([iubenda](https://www.iubenda.com/en/blog/gdpr-double-opt-in-2/)). Run double opt-in for everyone rather than segmenting.

**Penalties.** CAN-SPAM civil penalties up to $53,088 per individual email under the FTC's 2025 adjustment ([Adaptive Security](https://www.adaptivesecurity.com/blog/can-spam-act-requirements)). GDPR maximum €20M or 4% of global turnover. Per-email exposure is the point.

Also: a physical postal address in every email, a record of who consented and when, and never a purchased or scraped list.

## 6. What founders say they got wrong

**Hell Gate, on over-discounting.** A 99-cent promotion drove signups and unsustainable churn: "a discount can be so steep that it brings in people who don't actually value our journalism" ([Hell Gate 2025 annual report](https://hellgatenyc.com/hell-gate-2025-annual-report/)). Their numbers are a useful city-publication benchmark: 9,000+ paid subscribers, monthly recurring revenue from $42,000 to nearly $70,000, about $850,000 projected 2025 subscription revenue, against about $81,000 a month in costs. Mix: 67% subscriptions, 27% donations, 6% advertising, and 0% public funding after a promised state tax credit never arrived. Do not budget money that has not arrived.

**Brian Morrissey, The Rebooting, on imitation.** Too many Morning Brew and Ben Thompson knockoffs. His test: remove the branding and you should still identify the brand ([The Rebooting](https://therebooting.substack.com/p/lessons-of-2023)).

**404 Media, on the paywall.** The hardest launch call. They stayed almost entirely free for five months, then explained the paywall publicly so readers would evangelise it.

## What this changes for Vaya, in order

1. **The parking page is the launch list.** Replace the Squarespace page with the manifesto and one email field the day Pages goes live. Every outlet above did this months ahead.
2. **Launch as a cluster.** Four films, newsletter issue one, and the services page on one day. Not a trickle.
3. **Email authentication before the first send.** SPF, DKIM, DMARC, one-click unsubscribe, double opt-in.
4. **A standards page before the first film,** including the disclosure line no template covers: what Vaya's relationship is to any business on screen.
5. **Insurance before the first tour.** General liability for experiences, media liability for the films. Waivers do not replace either.
6. **Disclosure in the same format as the endorsement.** Spoken on camera, not only in the description.
7. **Do not discount to fill the first tour.** Hell Gate's lesson: cheap acquisition buys churn.
