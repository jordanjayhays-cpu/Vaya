# Getting average Americans into Filipino businesses: the wall, the doors, and the sequence

Date: 2026-09-13. Jordan's goal: a few ordinary Americans put money into one Filipino business, jobs get created, the Americans earn passive income they could not get at home. Sources inline. **Not legal advice. This is the map you take to a US securities lawyer.** UNSURE = no verifiable figure found. Cost estimates are marked as estimates.

---

## The conclusion first

**What you described is a security, and there is no informal version of it.**

The Howey test: an investment of money, in a common enterprise, with an expectation of profit derived from the efforts of others. Yours is all three. **"Passive income" is the phrase that makes it one.** There is no structure where several Americans hand you money for a share of a Filipino business's profits and it is not a securities offering.

**But three doors are open, and one of them is much wider than you would guess.**

1. **18.5% of US households already qualify as accredited investors.** Not a tiny elite. Roughly one in five.
2. **Rule 506(c) lets you advertise the offering publicly**, which is exactly what a video-first business needs, and the SEC eased the verification burden in 2025.
3. **An issuer selling its own securities is not a broker.** If the vehicle is yours, you can sell it, within a narrow safe harbour.

**And the sequence in your own Puhunan notes is right and this document does not change it:** own money first, two or three deals, filmed, then the vehicle. Raising first is backwards and expensive.

## Part 1: The wall

### Wall 1: the retail-friendly exemption is closed to Philippine businesses

**Regulation Crowdfunding (Reg CF)** is the one built for ordinary people: up to about US$5M in twelve months, non-accredited investors allowed. It is the obvious fit and it does not work here.

To use Reg CF an issuer must be **"organized under, and subject to, the laws of a State or territory of the United States or the District of Columbia."** Foreign entities are flatly excluded ([InnReg](https://www.innreg.com/blog/regulation-crowdfunding-guide-for-startups), [eCFR 17 CFR 227](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-227)). A Filipino sari-sari store, food cart or manufacturer cannot issue under it.

**The workaround exists but is narrower than it sounds.** Two structures are recognised ([Crowdfunding Attorney](https://crowdfundingattorney.com/2021/07/30/using-reg-cf-to-raise-money-for-a-non-u-s-business/)):
- The foreign business **moves its whole operation and assets** into a US corporation, usually Delaware. Investors then hold the consolidated business even though operations stay abroad.
- The foreign parent forms a **US subsidiary to develop the US market specifically**, and investors hold the subsidiary.

**The pitfall is explicit:** a Delaware shell with no assets, employees or operations violates Reg CF principles, funding portals should reject it, and rational investors should refuse it. So you cannot simply wrap a Manila business in a Delaware box.

Reg CF also requires the offering run **through a registered funding portal or broker-dealer.** You cannot do it yourself off your own website.

*(Note: Regulation A, the larger sibling, additionally requires the issuer be managed in the US or Canada. Reg CF has no management-location test, only the organisational one.)*

### Wall 2: "average American" usually means non-accredited

Accredited status in 2026 means, most commonly: **US$200,000 individual income (US$300,000 with a spouse) in each of the last two years, or US$1M net worth excluding the primary residence.** Holding a Series 7, 65 or 82 also qualifies ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/building-blocks/accredited-investors), [Carta](https://carta.com/learn/private-funds/regulations/accredited-investors/)).

### Wall 3: somebody has to sell it, and that somebody is usually a broker

Covered in `research/fdi-philippines.md`. Transaction-based compensation is near-conclusive evidence of unregistered broker-dealer activity, and Exchange Act s.29(b) gives investors rescission rights when the deal sours.

## Part 2: The doors

### Door 1: accredited is a much bigger group than you think

**Around 18.5% of US households qualified as accredited investors in 2022**, up from about 1.8% in 1983 ([Wiley, Financial Planning Review](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/cfp2.70023), [SEC](https://www.sec.gov/files/accredited-investors-us-population.pdf)).

**Why it grew: the thresholds have not been inflation-adjusted since the early 1980s.** Adjusted, the US$200,000 income test would be over US$500,000 today.

**So the "average American with some extra cash" is often accredited and does not know it.** A dual-income professional couple clears the US$300,000 joint test. Anyone with a US$1M net worth outside their house clears the other. **Telling people they qualify is itself a piece of content, and it is the top of this funnel.**

### Door 2: Rule 506(c) lets you advertise

Regulation D **Rule 506(c) permits general solicitation and general advertising** as long as every purchaser is accredited and the issuer takes reasonable steps to verify it.

**This is the door that fits a video company.** You may make the film, post it publicly, and point it at the offering. Under 506(b) you could not.

**And verification got easier.** SEC guidance and a no-action letter in March 2025 confirmed that **a high minimum investment amount can itself be a reasonable verification step** ([Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2025/03/new-sec-guidance-eases-burden-in-rule-506c-accredited-investor-verification-requirements), [K&L Gates](https://www.klgates.com/Rule-506c-Unchained-The-SEC-Loosens-Requirements-for-Advertising-in-Private-Capital-Raises-3-27-2025), [Paul Hastings](https://www.paulhastings.com/insights/client-alerts/sec-provides-updated-guidance-reducing-burden-for-rule-506-c-verification-requirement)). Otherwise verification means reviewing W-2s, 1099s, bank and brokerage statements, or a written confirmation from a broker-dealer, RIA, attorney or CPA.

**Practical effect: set a minimum of US$25,000 to US$50,000 and the minimum does much of the verification work.** UNSURE what minimum the SEC guidance treats as sufficient; that is a question for the lawyer, not a number to guess.

### Door 3: be the issuer, not the broker

**An issuer selling its own securities is not acting as an agent for anyone else, so it is not a broker** ([Kelley Clarke](https://kelleyclarkelaw.com/can-you-sell-securities-without-registering-as-a-broker-dealer/), [Proskauer on Rule 3a4-1](https://www.proskauer.com/uploads/summary-rule-3a4-1-safe-harbor-for-sales-securities-by-officers-employees-other-associated-persons-of-issuer)).

Rule 3a4-1 is the safe harbour for a person associated with the issuer who participates in selling. The conditions that matter here:

- **No statutory disqualification**
- **No compensation based directly or indirectly on transactions.** A management fee is fine. A cut of what you raise is not.
- Either substantial other duties for the company plus **no more than one offering in any twelve months**, or restriction to ministerial and passive activity

**So the structure is: you are the issuer.** Not a matchmaker introducing Americans to Filipino businesses, which is broker activity. The vehicle is yours, the investors buy into your vehicle, and your vehicle deploys the capital.

## Part 3: The structure that could work

**To be built by a securities lawyer, not from this document.**

```
US accredited investors
        |  Reg D 506(c), advertised through Vaya films, high minimum
        v
A US vehicle (likely Delaware LLC) with real substance:
a manager, a mandate, books, a diligence process
        |  loans or equity, deployed by the manager
        v
Filipino operating businesses
        |  repayment or profit share
        v
Distributions to investors, less a management fee
```

**Why it has to be a vehicle rather than deal-by-deal introductions:** a fund is one offering that you issue. Deal-by-deal matchmaking is a series of transactions you broker. The first has a door. The second does not.

### What it costs before a single dollar arrives
**All figures are estimates, not quotes. Get real ones.**

| Item | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Securities counsel: formation, PPM, subscription docs, Form D | US$15,000 to 40,000 |
| Fund administration and annual K-1s | US$5,000 to 15,000 a year |
| Philippine counsel on the deployment side | US$2,000 to 5,000 |
| Accredited verification service, if not using the minimum-investment route | Per investor |

### The arithmetic that decides whether to do it at all
A US$200,000 fund cannot carry US$30,000 of legal. **At a 2% management fee, a fund needs roughly US$2M before the fee covers the compliance**, which is about 40 to 80 accredited investors at US$25,000 to 50,000 each.

**That is a real fundraise, not a side product.** Anyone who tells you otherwise is selling you something.

### What the investor is actually signing up for
Disclose all of it, in the documents and on camera:
- **Currency risk.** Returns are earned in pesos and paid in dollars.
- **Tax complexity.** Depending on structure, K-1s, possible Form 5471, and possible PFIC treatment. PFIC in particular is punishing and badly understood. **UNSURE which applies without knowing the structure; the lawyer and a CPA decide this together.**
- **Illiquidity.** No secondary market. Their money is locked for the term.
- **Real failure rates.** Your own Puhunan notes say roughly half of new micro-businesses fail. That number goes in the documents, not just the conversation.

## Part 4: The version with no licence at all

**The Kiva model.** Zero interest, no profit share, no expectation of return beyond principal. With no expectation of profit, Howey is not satisfied and it is not a security.

- **What you get:** ordinary Americans, any amount, no accreditation, no lawyer, next month. Real jobs created. Films that work.
- **What you lose:** the passive income. It is philanthropy with repayment, not investment.

**Do not dismiss it.** It is a legitimate product, it is proven at scale in the Philippines, it builds the audience and the track record that make the 506(c) vehicle fundable later, and it costs almost nothing to start. UNSURE on the compliance details of running a lending platform even at zero interest; ask the lawyer, because state money-transmitter rules can still bite.

## Part 5: The sequence

Your Puhunan notes already had this right.

| Phase | What | Why now |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Own money** | Two or three deals, your capital, people you or your family know. Watch a full repayment cycle. | Legal with no structure at all. Teaches everything. **This is the only phase you can start this month.** |
| **2. Film them** | The operator, the money, the weekly sales, the repayment. Warts included. | The films are the track record. They are also the only thing that makes phase 3 fundable. |
| **3. Philippine side** | Whether deployment needs a lending company licence under RA 9474, or whether equity avoids it | Determines whether the vehicle lends or invests. **UNSURE; Philippine counsel decides.** |
| **4. The vehicle** | 506(c), accredited only, advertised through the films | Only when phase 2 has produced proof and an audience |

**Trying to raise before phase 2 is backwards.** With no track record you are selling a promise, which is both the hardest sale and the one most likely to end in a complaint.

## Why this idea is worth the trouble

Run it against the three-way test in `fdi-projects-and-funnel.md` part 4. **It passes more cleanly than anything else discussed.**

- **The American investor** gets access to a return category genuinely unavailable at home: small-business credit risk at emerging-market rates, which US retail cannot normally touch.
- **The regular American** *is* the investor here.
- **The Filipino business** gets capital that does not exist at that ticket size, at terms better than a lender charging 1 to 3% a month, and hires people.

**And the media is the moat.** Nobody else can show an American investor the actual owner, the actual stall, the actual weekly sales. That is a differentiated fund. It is still a fund, and funds are regulated, which is the whole point of this document.

## The top three ways this goes wrong

1. **You take money before the structure exists**, from a friend, informally, "just to test it." That is an unregistered securities offering, and the friend who loses money is the one who complains. **There is no small version of this that is safe.**
2. **A deal fails and an investor says they were misled.** Half of new micro-businesses fail; your own notes say so. If that number is not in the documents and on camera, the failure becomes a fraud allegation instead of a disclosed risk.
3. **You are paid a percentage of what you raise.** The fee has to be a management fee on assets, never a cut of the raise. This one is in every document in this repo now because it is the one that ends businesses.

## Accept when

Two or three deals have run to full repayment on your own money, they are filmed, a US securities lawyer has written down which structure is available to you, and the arithmetic shows the fund is big enough to carry its own compliance. Then, and not before, you raise.
