# Vaya game plan: Airbnb meets Vox and Vice

Date: 2026-09-06. The plan for building Vaya as a media brand you can book. Companion docs: `media-playbook.md`, `how-they-started.md`, `vaya-series.md`, `manila-revenue-model.md`, `manila-food-tours.md`.

## The thesis

Vox and Vice make you want a city. Airbnb lets you book it. Nobody does both under one name, in one city, with the same people on camera and on the ground. Vaya does. **Every episode is bookable.**

Airbnb tried the media half and gave up: Neighborhoods guides in 2012, a magazine with Hearst from 2017 to 2020, both shut. Vox and Vice tried the commerce half and never owned the real-world layer. Time Out is the one that got there, by accident, forty years in. Vaya starts there.

## What we take from each, and what we leave

### From Airbnb
- **Curated supply, not open supply.** Airbnb Experiences launched in 2016 with hosts applying and Airbnb reviewing every listing. In 2025 it relaunched with "Originals" hosted by named people. Vaya goes further: ten hosts per city, hand-picked, each with a film.
- **The trust layer.** Reviews, a vetting bar, a guarantee. Airbnb added the US$1M Host Guarantee in 2011 after one bad incident. Vaya needs the small version on day one: a waiver, a guide ratio, a refund rule.
- **The take rate.** Airbnb charges Experience hosts a flat 20%. Vaya's white-label take is 20 to 30%, which is cheaper for a host than Viator or GetYourGuide and comes with a film.
- **Do things that don't scale.** In 2009 the Airbnb founders flew to New York and photographed hosts' apartments themselves. Bookings doubled. Vaya's version: the videographer films every host. The free professional film is the reason a host signs.

### From Vox
- **The named series with a named host.** Borders worked because Johnny Harris was the face. Vaya's correspondent is the face of every Manila series.
- **The explainer format.** One question in the title, answered with facts on screen, 8 to 15 minutes.
- **Hire the person with the audience.** Vox hired Ezra Klein and Josh Topolsky. Vaya's first correspondent should already have a following in Manila, even a small one.

### From Vice
- **Immersion.** Live it, film it, let the story come out in the edit. A Week In is pure Vice.
- **Local stringers.** Vice used people who lived there. Vaya's whole model is people who live there.
- **Brands fund the journalism.** Vice's Virtue agency paid for the newsroom. Vaya's sponsors and hosts pay for the films.

### What we leave behind
- Airbnb's open marketplace. A thousand mediocre listings is Viator. Vaya is ten great ones.
- Vice's cost base and Vox's ad dependency. Under five full-time people per city, no banner ads.
- Airbnb's magazine mistake: media as a marketing cost. At Vaya, media is the funnel and the product catalog at the same time.

## The model: three layers, one flywheel

**Watch.** The Vaya Series (seven series, see `vaya-series.md`). Free, on YouTube and the newsletter.

**Book.** Vaya Experiences and Vaya Living. Tours, nights, a concierge, housing referrals, an investor briefing. Every one delivered by a vetted local host under the Vaya name.

**Belong.** The newsletter (One City, Unpacked), monthly meetups, later a members' group.

The flywheel: an episode features a host, the host's experience is bookable under the episode, guests leave reviews and footage, the reviews and footage feed the next episode and the newsletter, the newsletter sells the next booking. Each turn adds a host, a film, and a hundred emails.

## Supply: the Vaya Hosts program

Who: guides (Venus, Mari, SALO), chefs, brokers, a visa consultant, a coworking, a coliving, a finance or property contributor. Ten in Manila for season one.

The bar, five checks: lives in the city, already delivers the thing (reviews or a track record), will appear on camera, agrees to Vaya's route or standard, passes a reference call.

The deal:
- Vaya designs the product and sells it. The host delivers.
- Net rate or flat fee for tours. Referral fee for services. Vaya keeps 20 to 30%.
- The host gets a professional film about them, a Vaya page, and bookings they didn't have to find. That is the pitch.
- Exclusivity only on the Vaya-designed route, not on the host's own business.

Onboarding, one week per host: reference call, route walk with the correspondent, film day, page live, first public date.

## Demand: who books and where they come from

- **Movers** (the core): "moving to Manila" search, expat Facebook groups, Reddit, the Starter Kit.
- **Visitors** (the volume): YouTube suggested traffic on Eat Here, Not There and After Dark, hotel and hostel concierges, the guide's existing platform listings pointing at the Vaya route.
- **Investors** (the margin): The Read, LinkedIn, Placewell's network, IE alumni.

Owned channels first (newsletter, site), rented second (YouTube, Instagram), bought never in year one.

## Product and tech: build nothing you can rent

- The site stays static. Hibiscus theme, one page per city, one page per experience.
- Booking: a white-label widget (Bokun or Regiondo) or, to start, a payment link and a calendar. Do not build a marketplace.
- Newsletter: a provider with a form endpoint (Buttondown recommended).
- Reviews: collected by email after each booking, published on the experience page by hand until volume justifies a tool.
- Later, if the flywheel turns: a Lovable app for bookings and the members' group. Not before month six.

## Economics, with the assumptions showing

Per tour, assumption: PHP 3,500 a head, 8 guests, PHP 28,000 gross. Guide fee PHP 8,000, food and venue costs PHP 6,000, Vaya keeps about PHP 14,000 (50%) when sold direct. Sold through a platform at 25% commission, Vaya keeps about PHP 7,000.

Month-six target, assumption: four public tour dates a week across food and nights at 6 guests average, that is about PHP 300,000 gross a month, about PHP 120,000 to 150,000 to Vaya. Add affiliates, one sponsor, ten investor reports. That covers a correspondent and a videographer in Manila with something left. Test it against the first four weekends and rewrite.

What it does not cover: Jordan's time, equipment, travel. Those come from the BD agency until month six.

## Team

- **Jordan:** signs hosts, sponsors, brokers, affiliates, the first corporate client. Writes The Read. Sells. Does not guide, film, or deliver.
- **Correspondent-host:** the face. Verifies every guide, hosts every series, co-designs routes. Gets a piece of the upside (Puck's model) so they don't leave (Vox's mistake).
- **Videographer:** films hosts, tours, episodes. Freelance first, retained by month three.
- **Ops coordinator (month four):** dates, guests, guides, reviews, WhatsApp groups. Part-time local.
- Everyone else is a host or a freelancer.

## Phases

### Phase 0, now to day 30: the funnel and the first host
- Wire the forms. Site live at vayanews.com.
- Sign Venus (or Mari). Walk and film the Makati to BGC food route.
- Three affiliate programs live on the Manila page.
- First public tour date sold. Six guests.
- Correspondent identified, terms drafted.
- Metric: emails captured, one tour run, one host film in the can.

### Phase 1, days 30 to 90: the slate and the second product
- Film season one: The Real Cost, Block by Block (Poblacion), A Week In.
- Launch After Dark on the same guide. Weekly public dates for both tours.
- Three brokers under referral agreements. First investor report sold.
- First meetup, one venue sponsor.
- Newsletter weekly, referral ladder paid in tour discounts.
- Metric: bookings per week, subscribers, revenue per host, one sponsor conversation.

### Phase 2, days 90 to 180: ten hosts, sponsors, concierge
- Ten Vaya Hosts on the page, each with a film.
- First paid sponsor on A Week In or the newsletter.
- First concierge client delivered by hand, then decide service versus referral.
- One corporate relocation client via Placewell.
- Metric: monthly gross, Vaya margin, repeat and referral rate, sponsor revenue.

### Phase 3, city two: only when Manila passes the test
Three revenue lines each paying for themselves, a correspondent who could run Manila without Jordan, and a written playbook (host bar, route template, film template, deal template). Then Mexico City or Bali, same slate, same page template, a local correspondent hired first.

## Rules

- Curate, never list. Ten hosts, not a hundred.
- Every recommendation on camera is true with no deal attached. Links follow, never lead.
- Sponsored episodes under a quarter of the slate.
- No P. Burgos, no go-go bars, a female-guide option on every night product.
- Host-led. The correspondent owns the audience and shares the upside.
- One city until the test passes.

## Top 3 ways it goes wrong

1. **Building the marketplace before the supply.** Months on a booking app, three hosts on it. Fix: payment links and a calendar until ten hosts and fifty bookings say otherwise.
2. **The correspondent walks with the audience.** Vox lost Harris, Abram and the rest. Fix: equity or revenue share in writing before the first film.
3. **Jordan on the ground instead of on the phone.** Guiding and filming feel like progress. Fix: Jordan's week is hosts signed, sponsors pitched, brokers agreed. The number on the wall is signed hosts, not episodes.
