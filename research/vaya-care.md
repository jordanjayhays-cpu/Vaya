The headline, at roughly PHP 57 to the dollar (verify the rate on the day): a full-time licensed private nurse in Manila costs about US$500 to 800 a month at market salary. A live-in caregiver about US$400 to 450 a month. The same coverage in the US runs US$4,000 to 10,500 a month. **A retiree can have a full-time licensed nurse in Manila for less than one week of live-in care in the US.**

Say it carefully. The video's point is not "cheap labor." It is that a Western pension buys a level of care at home in Manila that is out of reach at home in Ohio, and that the nurse is paid at or above Manila market rate, through a licensed agency, with benefits. Vaya's voice is the friend who tells the truth, and the truth includes what the nurse earns.

## The products

### Vaya Care (for retirees and long-stay)
- **Live-in caregiver**, full time. For a retiree who needs daily help, not medical care.
- **Private nurse**, day shift or live-in. PRC-licensed, for medication, post-surgery, chronic conditions.
- **Care assessment**, one visit by a nurse to set the plan. The entry product.
- **Respite and short-term**, by the week, for a visitor's parent or a post-op fortnight.

### Vaya Staff (for movers, nomads, corporate)
- Driver (this replaces Model B in `manila-month.md`; Placewell as the employer of record is cleaner than a manpower agency Vaya doesn't know).
- Housekeeper, cook, nanny, personal assistant, English-speaking.
- Sourced, screened, and employed through Placewell; scheduled and sold through Vaya.

## Rules

- Licensed only. Nurses hold a PRC license, verified. Caregivers hold a TESDA NC II or equivalent. UNSURE whether Placewell's domestic placements fall under DOLE private employment agency licensing rather than the overseas (DMW) license; confirm which license covers in-country placement before the first contract.
- Placewell or the client is the employer, never Vaya. Vaya sells, schedules, and manages the relationship.
- Pay at or above market with benefits, in writing, shown to the client. The comparison video only works if the worker's side is honest.
- Medical boundaries. A private nurse works under a client's doctor. Vaya does not provide medical advice. The care assessment sets a plan and refers to a clinic.
- Backup cover. Every live-in placement has a named relief worker for days off.

## Top 3 ways it goes wrong

1. **The "cheap Filipino labor" framing.** One clip cut the wrong way and the brand is the villain. Fix: the worker's pay is on screen, the agency is named, the comparison is about what a pension buys, not what a person costs.
2. **A medical incident with an unlicensed carer.** Fix: PRC license verified for every nurse, a doctor of record for every care client, and a written scope of care.
3. **Vaya becomes the employer by accident.** Scheduling, paying, and directing the worker directly makes Vaya the employer in the eyes of the law. Fix: Placewell contracts, Placewell pays, Vaya manages the client.
