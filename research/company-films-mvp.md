# The MVP: company films. What it is, who it's for, and how to pick them.

Date: 2026-09-13. The end of the financing thread. Everything in `retail-investor-vehicle.md` parts 1 to 8 is the map of where this can go. **This document is the only part you do first.**

---

## The MVP in one sentence

**Vaya films Filipino companies that already have revenue, gets paid cash by the company, and does it ten times before touching equity, revenue share, or anyone's investment money.**

No lawyer. No entity. No securities. No platform. No flip. Revenue in the first month.

## Why this and not the rest

Every other rung needs something you do not have yet: a securities lawyer's answer, a Filipino company willing to restructure, an audience, a track record. **This rung needs a camera and an email.**

It is also not a detour. Ten company films produce, at the same time: revenue, the portfolio that makes media-for-equity credible, the relationships that surface the one company worth taking a revenue share in, and the content that builds the audience any future raise would need. **The MVP and the long game are the same activity.**

## The product

| | |
|---|---|
| **What** | One film about a Filipino company: the founder, the product, the numbers they are willing to show, a real day |
| **Who pays** | The company. Cash, invoiced, half up front |
| **Price** | PHP 60,000 to 150,000 (about US$1,050 to 2,600), by scope |
| **Cost** | About US$500 for videographer plus edit, per the existing rate in `DECISIONS.md` |
| **Delivers** | One 6 to 10 minute film, three vertical cuts, stills. Vaya publishes it and the company may use it anywhere |
| **Rights** | Work for hire, Vaya owns the footage, company gets a perpetual licence |
| **Legal exposure** | None. This is a production company selling production |

**Name, three versions:**
- **Safe: The Company Film.** Says exactly what it is, sells itself to a founder in four words.
- **Bold: Puhunan.** The strand name doubles as the product. Tagalog for the money you start a business with, so it means something to the customer in a way English does not.
- **Weird: Show Your Work.** Names what the founder actually has to agree to, which is the hard part of the sale.

Recommendation: **Puhunan** as the strand, **The Company Film** as the product a founder buys.

## Who to cover: the five-part filter

A company qualifies only if **all five** are true.

1. **It has revenue now.** Not a pitch, not a pilot. There has to be a number you can put on screen.
2. **The founder will be on camera and say real numbers.** No proxy, no marketing manager, no "we'd rather not disclose." **This is the one that disqualifies most of them, so ask it first.**
3. **There is something to look at.** A kitchen, a factory floor, a farm, a delivery route, a queue at a counter. A dashboard is not a film.
4. **They can pay PHP 60,000 to 150,000.** If they cannot, they are not the customer yet. Do not discount, per the playbook.
5. **Their constraint is attention, not capital.** A company that needs money needs a different thing. A company that needs to be known needs you.

### Disqualifiers, any one of them
- Pre-revenue
- The founder will not show numbers
- Regulated in a way that makes a claim on camera risky: lending, insurance, crypto, supplements, anything medical
- On the Foreign Investment Negative List, since Vaya would then be promoting to foreigners something they cannot own
- **They ask you to help them raise as part of the deal.** That is the compensation trap from `fdi-philippines.md`. The film is sold on its own or not at all

## Who to cover: the list

**Tier A. Start here. Filmable, solvent, and they already buy marketing.**

| Type | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Food and beverage brands with supermarket distribution** | Hot sauce, snacks, condiments. Revenue, visual, and a built-in Fil-Am export ambition that connects to the *Coming Home* audience |
| **Specialty coffee roasters and craft producers** | The most filmable businesses in the country. Owner-operators who love explaining their work |
| **Small manufacturers** | Furniture, ceramics, leather, textiles. A factory floor is footage before you point the camera |
| **Restaurant groups with two to five locations** | Have money, have a founder story, want covers. The easiest first sale |
| **Farm-to-brand agri** | Mango, cacao, coconut. Best visuals available and a real export story |

**Tier B. Good, harder to shoot or to sell.**

| Type | Note |
|---|---|
| **Logistics and delivery** | Motorbikes and warehouses shoot well; the sale is longer |
| **Clinics, gyms, wellness** | Already buy marketing. Watch the claims rule |
| **Fashion and homeware** | Filmable but crowded, and margins are thin |
| **Tech with a physical footprint** | Fintech serving sari-sari stores, agri-tech with actual farms. **The physical footprint is the only reason it is filmable.** Pure software is not |

**Tier C. Not at MVP.** Pure SaaS and B2B software, anything pre-revenue, crypto, lending, insurance, supplements, and anything that needs a lawyer before the first film.

### The single best first customer
**A food or beverage brand with supermarket distribution and an ambition to reach Filipino-Americans.** It clears all five filters, it can pay, it is visual, and it wants exactly the audience Vaya is already building. Start there and the first sale is the easiest one you will ever make.

## The plan

| Step | What | When |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Build a list of 30 companies against the filter. Desk research from Madrid: supermarket shelves, Shopee and Lazada brand stores, Facebook and Instagram, the DTI and Go Lokal directories | Now, from Madrid |
| 2 | Send the same short email to all 30 the same day | Now |
| 3 | Take the first three who say yes and can pay | Now |
| 4 | **Shoot them in the landing weeks.** The videographer is already booked and paid for the Vaya slate. Three or four paid company films in the same weeks means the trip pays for itself | January |
| 5 | Publish, invoice, count. Ten films before any equity or revenue-share conversation | Through year one |

**Ten is the number** because it is the playbook's own gate. Below ten you are guessing; at ten you know your price, your close rate, and which tier actually pays.

## The top three ways this goes wrong

1. **You take equity or a revenue share on film one** because a founder cannot pay cash and it feels like a deal. It is not a deal, it is an unpriced bet before you know your own price. **Cash for the first ten. No exceptions.**
2. **You agree to help them raise.** Even informally, even for free. It converts a clean production sale into a securities question. If they ask, the answer is: the film is the film, and what you do with it is yours.
3. **You film a founder who will not show numbers**, and produce a brochure. That is not a Vaya film, it fails the brand rules, and it will not sell the next one. **Ask the numbers question in the first email, not on the shoot day.**

## Accept when

Three Filipino companies have paid cash for a film, all three founders said a real number on camera, and you can state your close rate out of thirty emails.
