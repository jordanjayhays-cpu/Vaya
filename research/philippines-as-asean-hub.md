# The Philippines as a Southeast Asia hub for US companies

Date: 2026-09-13. Written after Jordan asked whether a US company doing well at home could use the Philippines as its base to serve the rest of Southeast Asia. Short answer: yes, but not for the reason most people assume, and not for every kind of company. Sources inline. **Not legal or tax advice.** UNSURE = no verifiable figure found or the figure is unstable.

---

## The conclusion first

**Do not sell the Philippines as a cheap factory. It will lose that argument to Vietnam every time and the client will find out.**

Sell it as three things it actually wins:

1. **A duty-free door into 600M+ ASEAN consumers.** Under ATIGA the vast majority of intra-ASEAN tariff lines are at **0%** for goods meeting the rules of origin, which is **40% ASEAN regional value content or a change in tariff classification**, whichever suits ([TTFC Law](https://ttfc.law/blog/2026-03-01-philippine-free-trade-agreements-foreign-investors/), [Carra Globe](https://carraglobe.com/reduce-import-duty-philippines/)).
2. **A 115M-person domestic market that is young, English-speaking and brand-receptive.** For a consumer brand the Philippines is not the hub, it is the *anchor market*, with ASEAN as the upside.
3. **The regional office nobody else can staff.** English, US business culture, US accounting familiarity. This is the one where the Philippines has no real competitor in the region.

**The reframe that matters: do not fight Vietnam for the factory. Take the office.**

## Why "cheap factory" is the losing pitch

| Weakness | The number |
|---|---|
| **Electricity** | Industrial end-user rates over **PHP 12 to 14 per kWh**, among Asia's highest. Vietnam lets factories bypass the state grid on private wires, cutting industrial power to a fraction of Philippine rates ([IBS Electronics](https://www.ibselectronics.com/resources/news/philippine-electronics-exports-could-break-$50-billion-in-2026-but-energy-costs-are-a-risk/), [Philstar](https://www.philstar.com/business/2026/09/09/2554898/expensive-electricity)) |
| **Ports and logistics** | Repeatedly named by investors as a disadvantage against neighbours. Island geography adds a domestic leg before anything reaches a port ([Indoneo](https://www.indoneo.com/capital/philippines-170-billion-infrastructure-fdi-race-vietnam-malaysia/)) |
| **Component supply chain** | Not integrated the way China's is. Inputs get imported, which erodes the labour saving |
| **Timing** | Vietnam and Malaysia captured the earlier waves of the China-plus-one shift and are consolidating. The Philippines is building ports and power plants against a head start |

**And the tariff advantage people assume exists, does not.** Negotiated framework rates: **Philippines 19%, Thailand 19%, Vietnam 20%.** Essentially identical. There is no arbitrage in manufacturing in Manila to sell into the United States.

> **UNSURE, and this one is dangerous.** The IEEPA reciprocal tariffs were struck down by the US Supreme Court on 20 February 2026 and replaced by a temporary Section 122 global tariff of 10% that was set to expire around 24 July 2026 ([TariffWise](https://tariffwise.co/news/reciprocal-tariffs-2026/), [Dimerco](https://dimerco.com/us-tariff-update-2026/)). What is actually in force today is unclear from these sources. **Never publish or quote a tariff rate without checking it the week you publish.** This is the single most volatile number in the entire Vaya Desk product.

## Why it wins anyway, for the right company

### 1. Electronics and semiconductors are already here

This is not a pitch, it is the existing reality and most Americans do not know it.

- Philippine semiconductor and electronics exports hit **US$49.64 billion in 2025**, up **16.11%**, and SEIPI expects them to break **US$50 billion in 2026** ([IBS Electronics](https://www.ibselectronics.com/resources/news/philippine-electronics-exports-could-break-$50-billion-in-2026-but-energy-costs-are-a-risk/))
- It is the country's largest export category by a wide margin
- The specialism is **OSAT: outsourced semiconductor assembly, test and packaging**, not fabrication. Trade friction is pushing OSAT out of Taiwan, China and Korea into Malaysia, Vietnam and the Philippines ([OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/promoting-the-growth-of-the-semiconductor-ecosystem-in-the-philippines_01497fea-en/full-report/examining-the-domestic-philippine-semiconductor-ecosystem-with-a-focus-on-assembly-testing-and-packaging_be2bc7eb.html))

**So on Jordan's chip example:** a fab will not come, power cost kills it. **Assembly, test and packaging already came, thirty years ago.** The pitch is joining an existing cluster with trained people, not building one.

### 2. PEZA makes the export math work

For a company producing for export, PEZA registration exempts **capital equipment, raw materials, spare parts and accessories from import duties and VAT** ([Emerhub](https://emerhub.com/philippines/peza-registration-in-the-philippines/)). Combined with the export-enterprise rule, a 60%+ exporter can be **100% foreign-owned outside the US$200,000 domestic-market capital threshold** entirely.

PEZA approved **PHP 140.69 billion in the first half of 2026, up 94%**, and its 2026 growth is planned as 60% manufacturing, 25% ecozone development, 15% IT-BPM.

### 3. The domestic market is the point for consumer brands

**On Jordan's soda example:** a beverage brand does not need cheap power or a deep component chain. It needs 115 million young consumers, a bottler, and distribution. The Philippines has all three, and ATIGA then opens Indonesia, Vietnam, Thailand and Malaysia duty-free from the same plant.

For consumer goods the Philippines is arguably the **best** ASEAN entry point, because it is the only large market in the region where an American brand's English packaging, English advertising and US cultural reference points work unmodified.

### 4. The regional office is the uncontested win

Nobody talks about this and it is the strongest card.

A US company expanding into ASEAN needs finance, HR, customer support, compliance and controls for the whole region. Those functions need **English at a native standard, US accounting familiarity, and US working culture.** Vietnam, Thailand and Indonesia cannot staff that. The Philippines has done it for thirty years at US$40B a year.

**The winning structure to sell:** manufacture wherever the physics says, which is often Vietnam, and put the **regional services, controls and customer-facing office in Manila.** That is honest, it is right, and it is precisely what Placewell delivers.

## The fit test

| Good fit for a Philippine hub | Bad fit |
|---|---|
| Consumer brands anchored on the domestic market | Fabs and anything power-hungry |
| Electronics assembly, test and packaging | Bulk or heavy goods with thin logistics margins |
| Medical devices and precision assembly | Apparel and textiles at scale. Vietnam won this |
| Regional shared services, finance, HR, support | Anything competing purely on unit cost with Vietnam |
| Anything where the customer speaks English | Anything needing a deep local component chain |
| Software, data and AI-services delivery | |

**One rule:** if the deciding variable is electricity or a port, the answer is Vietnam and Vaya should say so. Being the outlet that talks a client *out* of the Philippines once is what makes the next recommendation worth paying for.

## Where the three-way test lands

The three-way test in `fdi-projects-and-funnel.md` part 4 was written for consumer-facing projects and it does not transfer cleanly here. B2B expansion rarely has a "regular American" beneficiary.

**The B2B version is a two-way test:** the US business gets capacity or a market it could not otherwise reach, **and** the Filipino side gets jobs at or above market with skills that stay. A regional office in Makati passes easily. A bonded assembly plant that imports every input and exports every output, employing minimum-wage line workers with no skill transfer, does not.

## The top three ways this goes wrong

1. **Vaya quotes a tariff rate that has changed.** The US tariff position moved by Supreme Court ruling in February 2026 and by expiring temporary measures since. A client makes a siting decision on a stale number and loses money. **Every rate carries the date checked, and anything over 30 days old gets rechecked before it goes in a paid brief.**
2. **Vaya oversells the Philippines and a client builds the wrong thing here.** The mitigation is structural: The Comparison product must be able to conclude "not here," and one published film has to actually reach that conclusion.
3. **Vaya is treated as having given professional advice it is not licensed to give.** A brief that reads like a tax opinion or a legal opinion is one. Every brief names the licensed professional who must sign off, and says plainly what it is not.
