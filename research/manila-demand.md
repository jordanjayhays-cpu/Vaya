# Manila for movers, retirees, and visitors: the demand research

Prepared for Vaya, 2026-09-06. Sources inline. UNSURE = no verifiable figure found. Companion to `game-plan.md` and `manila-revenue-model.md`.

## 1. International arrivals

**Philippines totals (foreign visitors plus overseas Filipinos):**
- 2023: 5.45M, beat DOT's 4.8M target. Foreigners 5,003,475; overseas Filipinos 447,082. Receipts PHP 482.5B ([DOT](https://beta.tourism.gov.ph/news_and_updates/dot-chief-philippines-surpasses-yearend-target-with-5-45-million-intl-visitor-arrivals-in-2023-intl-visitor-receipts-surge-at-php482-54-billion/), [BusinessWorld](https://www.bworldonline.com/economy/2024/01/02/566491/international-visitor-arrivals-hit-5-45m-in-2023/)).
- 2024: 5.95M, up 9.2%, far short of the 7.7M target. Receipts a record PHP 760.5B ([BusinessMirror](https://businessmirror.com.ph/2025/01/06/missed-goal-5-95-m-foreign-tourists-visited-phl-in-2024/), [PIA](https://pia.gov.ph/news/luzon/phl-hits-record-high-tourism-revenue-in-2024/)).
- 2025: 6,484,060 (5,940,975 foreign, up 0.27%; 543,085 overseas Filipinos). Receipts fell 9.5% to PHP 694B. DOT published no arrivals target for 2025 and pivoted to "quality tourism" ([IAG](https://asgam.com/2026/01/25/philippines-visitor-arrivals-rise-0-76-to-6484060-in-2025-after-strong-december-tourism-numbers/), [Manila Standard](https://manilastandard.net/business/314693545/foreign-visitor-arrivals-in-the-philippines-flat-at-5-94-million-in-2025.html), [Manila Bulletin](https://mb.com.ph/2026/01/20/dot-quality-tourism-not-arrivals-alone-defined-ph-gains-in-2025)).
- 2026 year to date (Jan to Aug): 4,112,488 foreign arrivals, up 3.7%. Target 6.4M to 6.8M, stretch 7M ([Context.ph](https://context.ph/2026/09/02/dot-targets-up-to-7-million-tourist-arrivals-in-2026/), [BusinessMirror](https://businessmirror.com.ph/2026/07/20/phl-visitor-arrivals-near-3-2m-in-1h-on-china-india-surge/)). 1H 2026 was 76.5% of 1H 2019. The 2019 record of 8.26M has not been recovered.

**Stay and spend:** 11 nights average in 2024 (9 in 2019); US$2,073 per arrival ([GMA](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/money/economy/932999/tourists-in-ph-spend-an-average-of-11-nights/story/)). ## 2. Digital nomads

**Visa:** Executive Order 86, signed 24 April 2025, created a Digital Nomad Visa: 12 months, renewable once, multiple entry; 18+, income only from abroad, health insurance, clean record, and a national of a country offering Filipinos a reciprocal nomad visa ([EY](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/philippines-announces-new-digital-nomad-visa)). No income threshold is in the order; the US$24,000 figure online is agency guesswork ([Genki](https://guide.genki.world/philippines-digital-nomad-visa/)). Status as of 30 August 2026: not on the DFA e-Visa portal, no reciprocity list, no launch date. Uptake: zero. Some outlets claim applications are open ([Daily Tribune](https://tribune.net.ph/2026/07/19/digital-nomad-visa-2)); the DFA portal contradicts this. Treat it as not existing until DFA lists it.

**Nomad List (nomads.com):**
- Manila: 3.37/5, rank 215, US$1,649/mo, 38 Mbps, safety "Bad", quality of life "Bad", about 2,400 nomads present ([nomads.com](https://nomads.com/digital-nomad-guide/manila)).
- Cebu: 3.4/5, rank 199, US$1,854/mo, 10 Mbps, safety "Okay", about 1,200 nomads ([nomads.com](https://nomads.com/digital-nomad-guide/cebu)).
- Siargao: 3.92/5, rank 44, US$1,461/mo, 38 Mbps, safety "Good", frequent power cuts ([nomads.com](https://nomads.com/digital-nomad-guide/siargao)).
- Dumaguete: UNSURE.

**Nomad population for the Philippines:** UNSURE. No agency or index publishes one.

**Coworking:** Metro Manila flexible workspace hit 267,000 sqm / 49,500 seats by 9M 2025, above pre-pandemic; operators include WeWork, Common Ground, KMC, IWG/Regus, Compass, Penbrothers, Acceler8, Clock In ([Colliers](https://www.colliers.com/en-ph/news/evolution-philippine-flexible-workspace-industry)). 1H 2026 net take-up doubled to 6,000 seats ([Manila Bulletin](https://mb.com.ph/2026/08/01/flexible-office-market-enters-a-new-phase-of-growth)). Named coliving operators in Poblacion: UNSURE; nomad reviews warn against living in Poblacion ([nomads.com](https://nomads.com/digital-nomad-guide/makati)).

**Internet:** Philippines fixed broadband 53rd of 154 in Ookla's March 2025 index ([DICT](https://ictstatistics.dict.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Internet-Speed-of-the-Philippines-ao-March-2025.pdf)). ## 3. Retirees

**SRRV:** About 59,000 to 60,000 active holders as of mid-2025. 3,850 approvals in 2024; 2025 target of 4,000 on track. Holders roughly 40% Chinese, 20% Korean, 15% Indian, under 10% American (mostly former Filipinos) ([TTG Asia](https://www.ttgasia.com/2025/10/30/foreign-retirees-drawn-to-philippines-as-retiree-visa-approvals-near-4000/), [ACCRALAW](https://accralaw.com/2025/10/22/updates-on-the-philippines-retirement-visa-program/)). PRA's CEO says it is now targeting Europeans, Canadians, and Americans and "no longer promoting too much in Asia."

**Rule changes effective 1 Sep 2025:** minimum age cut from 50 to 40. Classic deposit US$15,000 with pension or US$30,000 without at 50+; US$25,000 / US$50,000 at 40 to 49. Courtesy option US$1,500. Application fee up to US$1,500. Extra US$15,000 per dependent beyond two. Stricter documentation for Chinese applicants ([ACCRALAW](https://accralaw.com/2025/10/22/updates-on-the-philippines-retirement-visa-program/)).

**Rankings:** International Living 2026 top 10 has Thailand 9 and Malaysia 10; the Philippines is not in it ([International Living](https://internationalliving.com/the-best-places-to-retire/)). The Expatriate Group's Retirement Abroad Index 2026 ranked the Philippines 1 of 20 (score 78, Thailand 77) ([PNA](https://www.pna.gov.ph/articles/1277570)). InterNations Expat Insider 2025: 13th of 46, strong on ease of settling in, near bottom on quality of life ([Inquirer](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/2175213/why-expats-love-and-loathe-life-in-ph-survey)).

**Cost:** couple comfortable in Cebu or Dumaguete on US$2,000 to 2,500/mo; Metro Manila expat lifestyle US$2,200 to 4,000/mo ([Live and Invest Overseas](https://www.liveandinvestoverseas.com/country-hub/philippines/cost-of-living-in-the-philippines/)).

## 5. Manila versus the competition

Nomads.com: Bangkok 1 (US$1,648/mo, 22,800 nomads), KL 4 (US$1,837, 10,200), Da Nang 16 (US$916, 12,000), Mexico City 20 (US$3,539, 8,400), Canggu 156 (US$3,296, 15,000), Manila 215 (US$1,649, 2,400) ([nomads.com](https://nomads.com/digital-nomad-guide/bangkok)). Manila costs the same as Bangkok and gets a tenth of the nomads.

Numbeo 2026 mid-year cost index: Makati 41.2, Bangkok 41.4, Bali 37.3, KL 37.4, Manila 35.4, Cebu 33.5, Da Nang 26.3 ([Numbeo](https://www.numbeo.com/cost-of-living/region_rankings.jsp?title=2026&region=035)). Safety index: Makati 61.9, Bangkok 61.8, Cebu 49.6, Bali 49.0, KL 40.9, Manila 35.4, Mexico City 33.8 ([Numbeo](https://www.numbeo.com/crime/region_rankings.jsp?title=2026&region=035)). Traffic: Manila 40th worst globally, 143 hours lost per driver in 2025, up from 2024; Philippines the most congested country in Asia ([Top Gear PH](https://www.topgear.com.ph/news/motoring-news/tomtom-traffic-index-2025-metro-manila-a6888-20260122)).

Why people choose Manila: English, easy settling in and social life (InterNations), 30-day visa-free entry extendable for up to 36 months, the cheapest retirement visa in the region. Why they avoid it: traffic, safety perception (Korean embassy advisories after crimes against Koreans drove the 2025 Korean collapse, [Philstar](https://www.philstar.com/headlines/2025/05/20/2444330/safety-issues-spooking-south-korea-tourists)), pollution, higher airfares than Thailand or Vietnam ([Inquirer](https://globalnation.inquirer.net/304244/more-hassle-than-fun-philippines-struggles-to-draw-tourists)), and a nomad visa that never launched.

Rising or falling: falling among Koreans and Asian nomads, flat overall, rising among Americans, Canadians, Chinese, Indians, and Western retirees. Arrivals are 2M below 2019 while Thailand, Vietnam, and Malaysia have passed their 2019 peaks.

## 6. What it means for Vaya

1. **Biggest audience is the American visitor, not the nomad.** 818,318 US arrivals in eight months of 2026 versus about 2,400 nomads in Manila at any time. A large share are Fil-Ams and balikbayans visiting family. Content should serve "I am in Manila for two weeks and want more than the mall."
2. **Richest audience is the Western retiree.** SRRV needs only US$1,500 to 30,000; couples budget US$2,000 to 4,000 a month for decades; PRA is now chasing Europeans, Canadians, and Americans. Vaya can be the trusted English-language explainer PRA lacks.
3. **Fastest-growing segments are China, India, and Canada** (up 64%, 43%, 16% in 1H 2026). Canada is the most reachable for an English product; India is the sleeper.
4. **Do not build around the Digital Nomad Visa.** It has not opened 16 months after the order. Cover it honestly. "It still doesn't exist" will rank and build trust.
5. **Safety is the conversion killer.** Manila's Numbeo safety index is 35, Makati's is 62. Every piece of content should be neighborhood-specific: Makati, BGC, Rockwell, Ortigas, not "Manila." That framing is the product.
6. **Housing is a buyer's market.** 25% condo vacancy, 79,000 unsold units, Airbnb revenue down 19.5%. Movers can negotiate. A rent benchmark by building and district is high-value, low-cost content, and landlords and brokers will pay to be in it.
7. **Manila is the gateway even when it is not the destination.** Nearly every foreign arrival lands at NAIA. A "48 hours in Manila before Siargao or Palawan" product captures the traffic that skips the city.
8. **Positioning:** Manila beats Bangkok on English and ease of settling in, ties on cost, loses on traffic and street safety. Say it plainly. Vaya's reader is the person with a reason to be in Manila: family, a BPO or multinational job, retirement, cost.
9. **Products the data argues for:** a Starter Kit priced for retirees and corporate movers (visa, SRRV, bank, condo, healthcare), a neighborhood safety and rent index updated quarterly, and films built around the American and Canadian returnee, because that is who is arriving.
10. **Measure against the right baseline.** The Philippines will not hit 7M in 2026 (4.11M through August implies about 6.3M). Vaya should not promise a boom. It should promise the truth about a city that is cheap, English-speaking, and hard.
