# The Vaya Series, and the business behind each one

> **Superseded 2026-09-06 by `vaya-series-v2.md`**, which rebuilt the slate on the demand data in `manila-demand.md`. Kept for the change history.

Date: 2026-09-06. The slate for Manila season one, then the paid thing each series feeds. Names are working titles; Jordan picks. Episode lengths follow the media research: 8 to 15 minutes ages well on YouTube and connected TVs, shorts are trailers.

## The spine

**One City, Unpacked.** The weekly newsletter. Every series episode lands here first, with the paid thing next to it. This is the asset. The series are the reach.

## The slate

### 1. The Real Cost
- **The question:** what does a month in Manila actually cost?
- **Format:** one real resident, 30 days, every peso on screen. Three lifestyles across the season: lean, comfortable, BGC expat. Ends with the number and the receipt stack.
- **Cadence:** one per lifestyle per season, refreshed yearly.
- **Hook:** the number in the title. "Manila on PHP 60,000 a month."
- **The business:** the Manila Starter Kit (free, email capture), then Vaya Concierge. Sponsor slot for a bank, remittance or insurance brand once there are numbers.
- **First money:** email capture. Concierge upsell from day 60.

### 2. Block by Block
- **The question:** which neighborhood is for me?
- **Format:** one neighborhood per episode, one day, on foot. Who it's perfect for, who it isn't, what a one-bed actually rents for, filmed inside real units. Poblacion, BGC, Salcedo, Legazpi, Ortigas, Quezon City.
- **Cadence:** monthly.
- **Hook:** the verdict. "BGC is not for you if..."
- **The business:** housing referral. Vetted brokers, serviced apartments and colivings pay a fee on each signed lease. A coliving sponsors the episode set in its neighborhood.
- **First money:** three brokers under a written referral agreement before episode one airs.

### 3. The Setup
- **The question:** how do I actually get set up here?
- **Format:** the practical machinery in one sitting. Visa options, SIM and eSIM, bank account, internet speeds tested on camera, coworking compared. A checklist in the description.
- **Cadence:** one per season, updated when rules change.
- **Hook:** the mistake. "The visa mistake every expat makes in Manila."
- **The business:** the affiliate stack. eSIM, nomad insurance, coworking passes, a visa agent. Every recommendation is one Vaya would make with no deal attached. The link follows the recommendation.
- **First money:** three affiliate programs live before filming. Zero delivery cost.

### 4. Eat Here, Not There
- **The question:** where do locals actually eat, and where do tourists get fleeced?
- **Format:** one dish or one strip per episode. The tourist version, the local version, the price of both. Makati and BGC first, Binondo later.
- **Cadence:** twice a month. This is the volume series.
- **Hook:** the comparison. "Same dish, PHP 180 versus PHP 1,200."
- **The business:** Vaya Food Tour, Makati and BGC, the elevated version. Vaya designs the route, a licensed guide (Venus, Mari or SALO) delivers under the Vaya name. PHP 3,000 to 3,800 a head, 20 to 50% margin.
- **First money:** one guide signed, one Saturday, six paying guests. The film is shot on the tour and becomes the ad.

### 5. After Dark
- **The question:** where do I go out without getting burned?
- **Format:** one night, one route, rooftops to a BGC club. Prices, dress codes, what to skip. No P. Burgos.
- **Cadence:** monthly.
- **Hook:** the route. "Poblacion to BGC in one night."
- **The business:** Vaya Nights. Same guide, PHP 3,500 to 4,000 a head, plus venue perks (free entry, welcome drinks, some bar-spend commission).
- **First money:** one Friday, eight guests. Guide licensing question answered first.

### 6. The Read
- **The question:** is Manila worth putting money into?
- **Format:** a host plus one local finance or property contributor. Property by district, business setup, what's rising versus hype. Charts, not vibes.
- **Cadence:** quarterly.
- **Hook:** the contrarian call. "Why BGC condos are the wrong buy."
- **The business:** the Manila investor briefing. A paid report (US$49 to 99, assumption), a paid call (US$150 to 300, assumption), and market-entry BD leads for the family agency. The video is the trailer for the report.
- **First money:** ten copies of the first report sold to the newsletter and Placewell's network.

### 7. A Week In
- **The question:** would I actually be happy here?
- **Format:** one real resident, one normal week, the good and the chaotic and the unposted. The host stays in a coliving or serviced apartment for the week.
- **Cadence:** one per season.
- **Hook:** the person. "A week in Manila with a nurse from Cebu."
- **The business:** sponsor integration. The coliving or coworking pays in kind first (the week), then a flat fee once there are numbers. This is the one series where the film is the product.
- **First money:** one in-kind deal before the pilot is shot.

## Season one, in order

The board task says three films plus the masthead. Those three are:

1. **The Real Cost** (lean lifestyle), because it feeds the Starter Kit and every other series points back to it.
2. **Block by Block** (Poblacion), because housing is the first thing a mover asks and the referral line is ready to earn.
3. **A Week In**, because it is the emotional one and the sponsor pays for it.

**Eat Here, Not There** and **After Dark** start as soon as the guide is signed, because they are filmed on tours that are already earning. **The Setup** and **The Read** are desk-heavy and can be made in Madrid with a local contributor.

## Production rules

- One host across the slate. Trust attaches to a person. Give them a piece of the upside.
- 8 to 15 minutes. Shorts cut from the episode are the trailers.
- Same open every time: the question, the place, the number. Same close: the paid thing, the newsletter.
- Every recommendation on camera is true with no deal attached. The affiliate link follows it, never leads it.
- Sponsored episodes stay under a quarter of the slate.
