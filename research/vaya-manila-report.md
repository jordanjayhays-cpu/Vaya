# The Vaya Manila Report: a Coface for one city, graded from the street

Date: 2026-09-14. Jordan sent the Coface Philippines country risk file and said "this is a good example." It is. This is what it would look like as a Vaya product.

---

## What Coface actually does

| | |
|---|---|
| **Two headline grades** | Philippines: **Country risk B**, **Business climate B** ([Coface](https://www.coface.us/news-economy-and-business-insights/economic-risk-dashboard/country-risk-files/philippines)) |
| **Scale** | Eight tiers, A1 (very low) to E (extreme). A is split A1 to A4 to separate industrialised countries |
| **Coverage** | 160 countries, **13 sectors across six regions, assessed quarterly** |
| **Method** | Macro, financial and political data, their own payment-experience data, and financial statements from **over 6,000 listed companies** |
| **Structure** | Grade, business climate, a strengths list, a weaknesses list, then sector grades |
| **Business model** | **Headline free, detail paid.** The free file is the marketing for the credit insurance |

**Their Philippines weaknesses list:** inadequate infrastructure, low fiscal revenues at about 16% of GDP, governance shortcomings and high corruption perception, weak manufacturing diversification with dependence on imported energy and capital goods, 98% of imported crude from the Gulf, and the Marcos-Duterte political rupture undermining government effectiveness.

**Their strengths list:** a large young population, half under 25, English-speaking; RCEP, ASEAN and bilateral trade agreements; remittances at about 8% of GDP from diverse origins; a thriving BPO sector; and tourism.

## The gap, which is the whole opportunity

**Every line of that is true, correct, and useless to someone actually deciding.**

> "Inadequate infrastructure levels." "Business environment is mediocre." "Governance shortcomings."

**Coface grades a country from a desk in Paris using the financial statements of listed companies. Nobody grades a city from the street.**

| Coface says | Vaya says |
|---|---|
| "Inadequate infrastructure" | "Industrial power is PHP 12 to 14 per kWh. Here is a real bill. Vietnam lets you bypass the grid and we cannot" |
| "Business environment is mediocre" | "The SEC registration took nine days. The mayor's permit took six weeks. Here is the timeline, dated" |
| "Governance shortcomings" | "Here is what we were asked for, at which window, and what we did about it" |
| A country grade of B | **A grade for Makati, a different one for Quezon City, and the reason they differ** |

**That is the same position as the Unit Check: everyone else works from the brochure, Vaya goes and looks.** It is the one thing a one-person operation can do that an institution with 6,000 company filings cannot.

## The product: the Vaya Manila Report

**A quarterly, graded, ground-truthed report on one city. Free headline, paid detail.**

### The grades
Copy the shape, not the scale. Coface's A1-to-E is built for credit risk. Vaya's reader is deciding whether to move, hire, or open something.

**Grade each system A to E, and grade it by district, because that is the whole insight.** `manila-demand.md` already says the safety gap between "Manila" at 35 and "Makati" at 62 is the conversion problem. **A single city grade repeats Coface's mistake at a smaller scale.**

### What gets graded

| # | System | What the grade actually measures |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Housing** | Vacancy, asking versus closing rent, how long to find something decent. Feeds from the Rent Index |
| 2 | **Getting around** | Door-to-door times at real hours, not distances |
| 3 | **Safety** | By district, by time of day, with the incident basis stated |
| 4 | **Connectivity** | Measured speeds in real buildings, and how long installation actually took |
| 5 | **Healthcare** | What a consultation costs, what the wait is, which hospitals take which insurance |
| 6 | **Money** | Can a foreigner open an account, how long, what breaks |
| 7 | **Paperwork** | Visa, ACR, permits. Days to complete, from Vaya Desk's own logs |
| 8 | **Cost** | A real month for a real person, itemised |
| 9 | **Schools** | Places, fees, waiting lists |
| 10 | **Hiring** | What roles cost, how long to fill, from Placewell's own data |

**Ten systems, four districts to start: Makati CBD, BGC, Ortigas, Quezon City.** Forty cells. That is a quarter's work for one person with a camera, and it is genuinely defensible.

### Where the data comes from, and this is the point
**Vaya's own operations are the research instrument.**

- Vaya Desk logs how many days each filing actually took → the Paperwork grade
- Vaya Property's Rent Index → the Housing grade
- Vaya Concierge's driver logs → Getting Around
- Placewell's placements → Hiring
- Vaya Care's assessments → Healthcare

**Every service Vaya sells generates a data point nobody else has.** The report is a by-product of doing the work, which is why a competitor with a bigger research budget cannot copy it.

### The business model, copied from Coface
| | |
|---|---|
| **Free** | The headline grades, the strengths and weaknesses lists, one district in full. Published, ranks, builds the list |
| **Paid** | The full forty-cell detail, the methodology, the quarter-on-quarter movement. US$99 to 299 a copy, or a subscription |

### What it does for everything else
It is the **credibility asset**. A one-person operation selling a US$2,000 market-entry brief is a guy with opinions. **The same person publishing a dated, sourced, quarterly graded report on ten systems across four districts is a research house**, and the brief then sells itself.

## Be honest about the effort

Coface has seventy years and 6,000 company filings. Vaya has one person and a camera. **So go narrow and deep, not broad and shallow.**

- **Do not** grade thirteen industrial sectors. That is Coface's job and they are better at it
- **Do** grade the ten things a person moving to Makati actually decides on, by district, with the date checked on every number
- **Quarter one is four districts and maybe six systems.** Publish what is real and mark the rest "not yet measured." **An honest gap beats a guessed grade**, and saying "we have not measured this" is exactly the Vaya voice

## The top three ways it goes wrong

1. **A grade is wrong and someone moves on it.** Every cell carries the date, the method and the sample size. Anything not measured is marked not measured, never estimated into a grade.
2. **Someone pays to change a grade.** It will be offered. The rule from `real-estate-product.md` applies at full strength: **anyone who pays Vaya anything is out of the report, permanently.** Publish the exclusion list alongside the report.
3. **It becomes a research project instead of a business.** It is a by-product of operations. **If Vaya is not delivering services, there is no data and the report should not exist.** Do not build it before the services run.
