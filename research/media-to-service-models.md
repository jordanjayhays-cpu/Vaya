# Who else sells a service off the back of a newsroom

Date: 2026-09-19. Research for the homepage rewrite. The question: Vaya wants newsroom framing, problem first, then the thing that fixes it. Who already does that, and what happened to them?

Six companies. Two proofs, one near-twin, one incumbent to avoid becoming, one corpse, one set of numbers about local newsrooms.

---

## 1. Atlas Obscura — the proof, and the closest structural match

Started as an editorial site about strange places. Now describes itself as **"an experiences company that's powered by media."** That sentence is the whole thesis of Vaya.

| | |
|---|---|
| Trips and experiences | **60% of revenue** |
| Ads | under 50% and falling |
| Experiences run last year | about 450 |
| Five years earlier | 150 |
| Local experience price | US$25 to 55 |
| Multi-day trip price | US$3,580 (8 days, Sardinia) |
| First profit | **January 2026** |

**What Vaya takes:** the structure is validated. Editorial builds the audience, the experience takes the money, and the media keeps its independence because it is not the revenue.

**What Vaya has to swallow:** Atlas Obscura was founded in 2009 and turned its first profit in 2026. Seventeen years. They were venture-funded and building a global events operation; Vaya is one person with four products and a much lower bar. But nobody should look at that 60% and think it arrives quickly.

## 2. Nomad Capitalist — the funnel, at the extreme end

Andrew Henderson. Free YouTube about tax residency and second passports, then high-ticket consulting.

| | |
|---|---|
| YouTube subscribers | ~700,000 |
| YouTube ad revenue | ~US$10,000/month |
| Consulting revenue | ~US$175,000/month (estimated) |
| Package price | US$30,000 to 75,000 |

**The ratio is the lesson.** The content is roughly 5% of the money. The service is 95%. He gives away everything he knows on camera and charges only for what he does. That is `DECISIONS.md` 2026-09-14 arriving at the same answer independently.

**Where it differs:** he sells to the top few percent by net worth. Vaya sells PHP 2,500 visa filings. Same shape, two orders of magnitude apart on price, which means Vaya needs volume and a much cheaper delivery cost per customer.

## 3. Expatsi — the near-twin, and the most encouraging one

A couple who spent five years researching their own move abroad, then built a company for Americans wanting to leave the US.

- **Free 10-question quiz** asking where you want to live and why (study, work, retire), returns a shortlist of cities.
- **Paid scouting trips.** First one in March 2024: 30 Americans, 10 days in Portugal, 12 in Spain.
- Price: **US$1,000 Portugal leg, US$1,200 Spain leg.**
- On the ground: a seminar on visas, healthcare, finance and real estate, plus a local guide walking neighborhoods and showing actual units for rent and sale.

**This is Vaya Home — Move In and Vaya Tours, run as a group instead of one at a time.** Two people started it in 2024 and got national press (CNBC, CNN) inside a year. The free tool is the top of the funnel and the trip is the product.

**What Vaya has that they do not:** a correspondent who lives there. Expatsi's founders are visitors running a tour. Vaya's premise is the person is already local, which is the one thing a scouting-trip company cannot fake.

## 4. International Living — the incumbent, and the warning

45+ years publishing about retiring overseas. Revenue comes from subscriptions, country conferences and "boot camps," plus advertising and stakes in side businesses including real estate offerings and agricultural investments.

They are the closest competitor to Vaya's subject matter and they got there first.

**But:** customer reviews on Trustpilot and the BBB repeatedly describe relentless upselling and long sales emails. The reputation cost of monetizing trust is visible in public, in writing, for anyone to read.

**What Vaya takes:** this is precisely what `standards.html` and the flat-fee rule exist to prevent. Taking a cut of a real estate deal you wrote about is how a 45-year-old publication ends up with those reviews. Vaya already decided against it. This is the evidence that the decision was correct.

## 5. Culture Trip — the corpse

An SEO-driven destination content factory that raised **US$102 million**.

| | |
|---|---|
| Revenue, year to Sept 2018 | **US$659,000** |
| Loss, same year | **US$30.3 million** (up 159% on 2017) |
| Outcome | entire NY editorial team laid off 2020, sold to its own CEO 2023 |

They built content first at enormous volume, then tried to become an online travel agency, failed, then tried to become a small-group tour company.

**The lesson is the order of operations.** They had attention and went looking for something to sell. Vaya has the opposite problem and it is the better one: four defined products, no audience yet. Attention is cheaper to buy than a delivery capability is to build.

## 6. 6AM City — what a "local newsroom" actually costs

The most relevant numbers to Vaya's "a bureau in every city" claim.

| | |
|---|---|
| Revenue 2023 | ~US$8 million |
| Markets | 31+ |
| Daily readers | ~1.5 million |
| **Subscribers needed per market to be sustainable** | **50,000** |
| Margin once mature | 70% within 36 months |
| Funding raised | US$18.8 million |

**Read that 50,000 carefully.** That is the threshold for an *advertising-funded* local newsletter. It is the number Vaya would have to hit per city if the newsroom paid for itself with ads.

Vaya's four products carry PHP 1,000 to 35,000 of margin each. At PHP 710,000 of margin on roughly 300 to 500 transactions (`research/income-scenarios.md`), Vaya reaches a working year on **a few hundred customers, not fifty thousand readers.** That is the argument for never selling ads, stated in numbers.

## What all six say together

**1. The winners sell a service they perform. The loser sold attention.**
Atlas Obscura 60% experiences. Nomad Capitalist ~95% consulting. Expatsi sells trips. Culture Trip sold pageviews and died at US$659k revenue on US$102m raised.

**2. Free information is the top of every working funnel, not a product.**
Henderson gives away the tax strategy. Expatsi gives away the quiz. Atlas Obscura gives away the articles. Nobody charges for the knowing.

**3. Monetizing the trust is the failure mode, and it is visible from outside.**
International Living survived 45 years doing it and wears the reviews. Vaya's standards page is the differentiator, not a compliance chore.

**4. Editorial independence survives only if the money comes from somewhere else.**
Every one of these that kept its voice had a revenue line that did not depend on saying nice things.

**5. Nobody does this from inside the city.**
Expatsi flies in. International Living has correspondents but sells conferences. Atlas Obscura is global. The correspondent who lives there is the gap.

---

## What this means for the homepage

The pattern the working companies share: **name the problem in the reader's own words, show the thing you do about it, and let the film be the evidence rather than the pitch.**

Nobody leads with "we are a media company." Atlas Obscura leads with a strange place. Expatsi leads with a quiz. The service is one click away, never on the doorstep and never hidden.

For Vaya that means each of the four products should be introduced by the problem it solves, in the voice of rule 2 (a number beats an adjective):

| Product | The problem, stated | The thing |
|---|---|---|
| Vaya Desk — Visa | Your tourist visa expires and the nomad visa signed in April 2025 still does not exist | We file the extension. PHP 2,500 |
| Vaya Concierge — Landing | You land at NAIA at 11pm with no +63 number, so no Grab, no GCash, no Maya | Someone is at arrivals. PHP 3,000 |
| Vaya Tours — Food | You will eat where the algorithm sends you | A licensed guide who lives here. From PHP 3,000 |
| Vaya Home — Move In | Makati scores 62 on safety. Manila scores 35. The brochure crops the difference | Five filmed viewings, the lease read, keys that work. PHP 45,000 |
