# Outreach 09: the company film pitch, Vaya Studio

**Send to:** Filipino companies with revenue, a founder who will say real numbers on camera, something to look at, ability to pay PHP 60,000 to 150,000, and a constraint that is attention rather than capital. All five, or no film. Filter is in `research/company-films-mvp.md`.

**This is the funding mechanism, not a side line.** Each film is roughly PHP 52,000 of margin. Seven of them pay for a year of a videographer. `research/income-scenarios.md` calls selling one a month the single most valuable habit in the business.

**Send to the founder, not to info@.** If you cannot find the founder, the company is probably too big for this.

---

## The email

Subject: **A film about [company], and what it would cost**

Hi [name],

I am Jordan Hays. I run Vaya, a video newsroom in Manila.

I want to make a film about [company], and I am writing to ask whether you would pay for it.

Here is why I am asking you and not someone else.

[One specific, true sentence about their product or their story. Something you could only know by having looked. If you cannot write this sentence, do not send the email.]

What the film is.

Eight to twelve minutes. You, the place where the work happens, and the thing you make. Real numbers on screen, said by you.

Not an advertisement. If I make an advertisement nobody watches it, which is no use to either of us.

That means I will ask what it costs you to make, what you charge, what went wrong, and what you would do differently. You see the cut before it goes out and you can pull the whole thing. You cannot ask me to remove one inconvenient number and keep the rest.

What it costs.

[PHP amount]. That is a flat production fee. Two shoot days, the edit, and delivery of the long film plus short vertical cuts you own and can use anywhere.

What I do not take.

No equity, no commission, no share of anything the film brings you. A flat fee for work delivered, invoiced, done.

What you get that an agency does not give you.

The film goes out on Vaya's channel as editorial, not as a paid post, and it is labelled as paid production so nobody is misled. You also get the files to use yourself.

Two questions.

Would you be open to being filmed on that basis, including the numbers part?

And is [PHP amount] in the range you would consider for this kind of thing?

If it is easier to talk, I am on [number] or happy to do a call.

Thank you,
Jordan Hays
Vaya, Voices Around Your Area
vayanews.com

## Before sending

- **Write the specific sentence or do not send.** A founder can tell in three seconds whether you looked at their company. A generic pitch is worse than no pitch, because it burns the name
- **Fill in the price.** PHP 60,000 to 150,000 depending on the company. Pick one number and say it. Asking for their budget first loses you the anchor
- **Read `standards.html` before you promise anything.** Anyone who pays Vaya is excluded from any Vaya ranking or comparison, permanently. Say this on the call, not in the email
- **Never send it with a bracket in it**

## Top three ways this goes wrong

1. **They want approval over the final cut, not just a preview.** That is the negotiation. Offer factual corrections and the right to kill the whole film, never the right to remove one number and keep the rest. If they insist, walk. A film they control is an advertisement and is worth nothing to your audience
2. **They ask for a discount in exchange for exposure or a testimonial.** No. A flat fee or nothing. The moment the price moves for a non-cash reason, you are in a relationship rather than a transaction, and the next film is harder to charge for
3. **You sell it before you can deliver it.** Do not send this until you have a videographer and at least one film published that you would show a stranger. A pitch with nothing to link to converts at zero and costs you the contact
