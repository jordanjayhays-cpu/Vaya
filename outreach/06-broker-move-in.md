# Outreach 06: the licensed broker, for Vaya Home — Move In

**Send to:** three or four PRC-licensed brokers working Makati, BGC, Rockwell or Ortigas. Find them on Lamudi listings (the PRC number is on the listing), on LinkedIn, or through the Makati and BGC building administrators.

**The ask is unusual, so lead with it.** You want them to source options for your client, and you are not taking a peso of their commission. Most brokers have never had that conversation and it is why they will reply.

---

## The email

Subject: **Bringing you foreign tenants and buyers, no commission split**

Hi [name],

I run Vaya, a small media and services company covering Manila for people moving here from abroad.

I am setting up a service called Move In.

Someone moving to Manila pays us a flat fee to find them somewhere to live and get them settled, and we do everything except the transaction itself.

That last part is why I am writing.

We are not brokers and we do not want to be.

A licensed broker sources the options and handles the lease, and I want that to be you.

Here is what I would do, and what I would need.

What Vaya does: takes the client's brief, asks you for five options in the right district, visits and films every one, reads the lease and tells the client what to negotiate, then handles utilities, internet and the first week.

What you do: source the options, and handle the lease and the transaction exactly as you normally would.

What I am paid: a flat fee by the client, paid before we start, whatever they decide.

What you are paid: your normal commission, in full, from the lease or the sale.

I take nothing from you and you owe me nothing.

I also want your name and PRC number on anything we publish about a property, because the listing is yours and not ours.

Two questions.

Would you be open to sourcing options for a client of mine on that basis?

And are you comfortable being named and filmed, since we publish what we see, including the things a brochure leaves out?

If it is easier to talk, I am on WhatsApp at [number].

Thank you,
Jordan
Vaya
vayanews.com

---

## Notes before sending

- **Do not send this until the lawyer has confirmed the RESA position.** If a flat client-paid fee for finding and settling somebody is ever read as real estate practice, this email is evidence you were doing it knowingly. Ask first, send second.
- **Send to three or four, not one.** You want a choice, and the first broker who says yes is not necessarily the right one.
- **The filming question is the real filter.** A broker who will not be named on camera is not a partner, because half the product is showing what the listing actually looks like.
- Replace [number] before sending. **Never send it with a placeholder in it.**

## Top three ways this goes wrong

1. **They assume you want a referral cut and stop reading.** That is why the subject line and the fourth paragraph both say the opposite. If they still ask for a split, say no in writing and keep the email.
2. **They send you five listings that are all theirs, including the bad ones.** Expected. Your job is to film them honestly, which is exactly why the client is paying you and not the broker. Say so up front so nobody is surprised when a film says a unit is overpriced.
3. **They quietly list your client as their buyer with a developer** to lock the commission, which limits the client's options later. Ask how they handle buyer registration before you send them a single client.
