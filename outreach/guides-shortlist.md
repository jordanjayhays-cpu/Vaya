Several directory entries carry a "For Re-Accreditation" tag, which now reads as the honest ones.

**So the directory is a lead list, not a verification.** Every accreditation number must be checked on the guide's own profile page, and then confirmed with the guide, before Vaya puts a customer in front of them.

**This is a question for the lawyer hour:** is DOT accreditation legally required to guide a paid tour, or is it voluntary and reputational? The answer decides whether an expired number disqualifies someone or is merely untidy. **Do not assume either way.**

It also suggests an obvious thing to ask a guide on a first call: *"Is your accreditation current, and when did you last renew?"* An operator who renews on time is telling you something about how they run everything else.

## How to work the rest of the directory

About ninety guides on the English and Filipino page alone, plus separate pages for Mandarin, Japanese, German, Spanish, Russian, Arabic, Italian and Bahasa.

**The method:**
1. Open a profile. Check **Accreditation valid until** first. If it has passed, park it
2. Read the biography. You are looking for someone who **runs something**, not just guides for an agency. Founders have rate cards and opinions
3. Note the languages. A Japanese or Mandarin-speaking guide is a different product, not a backup
4. Only then look at the email

**Do not mass-mail this list.** These are individual freelancers with published contact details for the purpose of being hired by visitors. One considered email to three or four guides beats ninety identical ones, and the Manila guiding community is small enough that a spray would be noticed.
