# The newsroom: who delivers what, and the week that repeats

**Two contractors and Jordan.** `PLAYBOOK.md` section 6 sets the cadence: one newsletter a week, one film twice a month, a monthly review, a quarterly slate cut. **This document turns that into deliverables, because a contractor is engaged for outputs and not for hours.**

> **The rule that shapes everything below.** Both hires are **independent contractors**. So every row in this document is a thing delivered to a standard by a date, for a fee. **None of it is a schedule, a shift, or a task assignment.** They choose their own hours, use their own equipment, and keep other clients. If a process here starts reading like management, it is written wrong. Lawyer question 5 in `outreach/08` settles the detail.

---

## The split, stated once

| | **Correspondent (the lead journalist)** | **Videographer** |
|---|---|---|
| **Owns** | What the film says and whether it is true | What the film looks and sounds like |
| **Delivers per film** | The episode brief, the people, the fact-check sheet, the signed releases, the piece to camera | The footage to the kit, the two angles, the b-roll, the sound, the graded cut |
| **Delivers weekly** | The newsletter: one episode, one paid thing, every comment answered | Nothing weekly. Film-based |
| **Does not decide** | Price, product, who Vaya partners with | What the film is about |
| **Paid** | Flat fee per film plus a monthly retainer once Manila passes stage 5 | Per film, retained from film four |
| **Owns the copyright** | **No. Assigned in writing, or Vaya does not own its own work** | **No. Same.** `ops/videographer-engagement.md` |

**The one thing both own jointly: the disclosure line.** If a business is named and Vaya earns from it, one of them says so on camera. **Either of them can stop a shoot over it.**

## The film cycle: one film, five stages

A film takes about three weeks of calendar and roughly five working days of actual work. **Two films a month means two of these overlapping.**

| Stage | Deliverable | Owner | Gate before the next stage |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Brief** | `ops/episode-brief-template.md`, filled | Correspondent | **Jordan signs off the question and the code.** No brief, no shoot date |
| **2. Fact pass** | Every number, with its source and the date checked | Correspondent | **A number without a date does not go on screen.** `standards.html` promises this publicly |
| **3. Shoot** | Footage, two angles, b-roll, clean sound, **signed releases** | Videographer leads | **Offload to two drives before anyone goes home.** `ops/footage-pipeline.md` |
| **4. Cut** | Graded cut to the kit, graphics in, code on the receipt end-card | Videographer | **`ops/publish-checklist.md`, every line.** Jordan is the last read |
| **5. Publish** | Live, description link with the code, newsletter, comments answered | Correspondent | **Row in `ops/episode-tracker.csv` the same day** |

**Nothing skips a gate because a date is close.** The thing Vaya is selling is that it is accurate, and a rushed film is the one that breaks the thing that cannot be rebuilt.

## The week

**Written as what is due, not as what happens on which day.** The two contractors pick their own hours.

| Due | Deliverable | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| **Once a week** | **The newsletter.** One episode, one paid thing, the episode code in every link | Correspondent, Jordan reads before it sends |
| **Once a week** | **Every comment and every inbound email answered.** Including the ones that say Vaya got it wrong | Correspondent for comments, Jordan for anything about money |
| **Twice a month** | **One film published**, to the kit, with its code on the receipt end-card | Both |
| **Once a month** | **The review.** Revenue per episode code, ranked. Emails per code. One decision written to `DECISIONS.md` | Jordan runs it, both attend |
| **Once a quarter** | **The slate cut.** Kill the series earning least, double the one earning most | Jordan |

### The monthly review is the only meeting that matters

Three numbers, per episode code, and nothing else on the agenda:

1. **Views**, and how far through people got
2. **Emails**, counted by the code in the subject line
3. **Revenue**, from `ops/revenue-tracker.csv`

**If a film earned nothing, say so out loud in the room.** The slate is supposed to lose films. `FUNNEL.md` explains why the subject line is the only attribution Vaya has, which is why a row without a code teaches nothing.

## The three standing rules a contractor can enforce without asking

These exist so neither hire has to call Jordan to do the right thing.

1. **No release, no footage.** Either of them can refuse to use a shot. No escalation needed
2. **No date, no number.** A price goes on screen with the date it was checked or it does not go on screen
3. **No disclosure, no recommendation.** If a named business earns Vaya money and the line was not said on camera, the recommendation comes out in the edit

**All three are already promised publicly on `standards.html`.** Giving the team the authority to enforce them is what makes the page true rather than decorative.

## When something goes wrong in public

**The correction process, because `standards.html` promises it in detail and promises are cheap until tested.**

| Where the error is | What happens | Who | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| **In a film** | Correction in the description **and** a pinned comment. **If the error changes the answer, re-cut or re-shoot** | Correspondent writes it, videographer re-cuts | Within 48 hours of knowing |
| **In a guide or newsletter** | Correction at the top, dated. **Not a quiet edit** | Correspondent | Same |
| **In a price** | Fix it and say what changed | Correspondent | Same |
| **Anything about money, a partner, or a person** | **Jordan, before it is posted** | Jordan | Same |

**Nobody waits for permission to correct a fact.** They wait for permission only where money, a named person or a partner is involved.

## Accept when

Both contractors can run a full film cycle, publish it, answer the comments and log the row **without asking Jordan what to do next**, and Jordan's only involvements are signing off the question at stage 1 and being the last read at stage 4.
