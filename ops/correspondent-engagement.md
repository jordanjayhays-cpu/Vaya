# Correspondent engagement letter

**Send before the first shoot day, the first newsletter, or the first piece of writing. Whichever comes first.**

## Why this exists

**The same Philippine IP Code section 178.4 problem as the videographer's letter**, and `ops/videographer-engagement.md` only covers the camera. Where a work is commissioned by someone who is not the creator's employer and who pays for it, **the commissioner owns the work but the copyright stays with the creator, unless there is a written stipulation to the contrary.**

A correspondent produces **written work** (the newsletter, the guides, the episode briefs) and **a performance on camera**. Both carry rights. **Paying for them does not transfer those rights. A signed sentence does.**

**There is a second reason this letter matters more than the videographer's.** The correspondent is the face of the series and writes the weekly newsletter, so they look like staff. **They are not, and the paperwork has to say so clearly**, because `PLAYBOOK.md` pays them a monthly retainer once Manila passes stage 5, and a monthly payment to a named face is exactly the arrangement that gets re-characterised as employment with back pay attached. **This is lawyer question 5 in `outreach/08` and it is not settled yet.**

## The letter

Subject: **Engagement terms, Vaya Manila correspondent**

Hi [NAME],

Confirming what we agreed so we both have it in writing.

**The work.** You are the Vaya Manila correspondent. Per film: the episode brief, sourcing and briefing the people in it, the fact-check with every number sourced and dated, collecting signed releases, and appearing on camera. Weekly: the newsletter, and answering comments and reader email. **[NUMBER] films and [NUMBER] newsletters between [START] and [END].**

**The fee.** PHP [AMOUNT] per published film, plus PHP [AMOUNT] a month for the newsletter and reader replies. Invoiced monthly, paid within [N] days. Travel and anything you buy for a story is billed at cost with receipts.

**How we work together.** You are an independent contractor, not an employee. **You set your own hours and choose how you do the work.** You keep other clients and other bylines. We agree what is delivered and to what standard; we do not direct your working day. You use your own equipment and your own workspace.

**Copyright.** You assign to [ENTITY LEGAL NAME] all copyright and related rights in everything you write, record or present for us under this engagement, worldwide and for the full term of copyright, including the right to edit, publish, license and sub-license it. **This is an assignment of copyright, not only of ownership of the files.**

**Your performance on camera.** You consent to us filming you and using your name, likeness and voice in Vaya films, on the site, in the newsletter and on social, worldwide and with no time limit, in connection with this work.

**Your byline and your portfolio.** You are credited on everything you make for us unless you ask not to be. You may show the published work in your own portfolio and on your own channels. You may not republish the full text or license it to anyone else.

**Editorial standards.** Everything you make follows `standards.html`, and that page is part of these terms. **Three things in it you can enforce on your own authority, with no need to ask me:** no release means the footage is not used; a number with no date does not go on screen; a named business we earn from gets the disclosure line said on camera or the recommendation comes out.

**What we will not ask you to do.** We will not ask you to recommend anything in exchange for payment to Vaya, to leave out a disclosure, to publish a number you have not checked, or to drop a correction. **If I ever ask you for any of those, say no and send me this paragraph.**

**Corrections.** When something we published turns out to be wrong, we correct it in public, dated, where it appeared. That is not optional and it is not a failure.

**Confidentiality, in one direction.** Partner rates, customer details and unpublished work stay between us. **This does not restrict what you may say about working for Vaya.**

**Ending it.** Either of us can end this with [30] days' notice. Films already shot get paid. Anything published stays published.

Reply with "agreed" and your full legal name and address and that is enough for me.

Thank you,
Jordan
vayanews.com

## Before you send

- **Fill every bracket.** A letter with `[ENTITY LEGAL NAME]` in it is worse than no letter
- **The lawyer reads this one before the videographer's**, because the contractor-versus-employee risk is higher here. It is lawyer question 5 and this letter is the exhibit
- **Two numbers are not decided yet:** the per-film fee and the monthly retainer. **The drafted PCIJ email in Gmail asks exactly this question**, which is one more reason to send it
- **UNSURE** whether a reply saying "agreed" satisfies the written stipulation requirement under s.178.4, or whether a signature is needed. Same open question as the videographer's letter. **Ask the lawyer once, for both**

## Top three ways this goes wrong

1. **You pay a monthly retainer to the face of the series and direct their week.** That is an employee in substance however the letter is worded. **The letter has to be true in practice**, which is why `ops/newsroom.md` is written as deliverables and not as a schedule
2. **You send it after the first shoot.** Then the copyright in the footage of your own correspondent sits with them until they sign, and the leverage has moved
3. **The standards paragraph is treated as boilerplate.** It is the one clause that gives a contractor the standing to refuse an instruction from you. **That is the point of it.** If it reads as decoration, it will not be used on the day it is needed
