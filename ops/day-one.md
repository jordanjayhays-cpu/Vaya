# Day one with the correspondent and the videographer

**The first day all three of you are in the same room.** Half of it is setup, half of it is shooting something real, and the day ends with footage on two drives and one thing published.

**Why it ends with something published.** A day one that produces nothing teaches the team that Vaya talks. Shoot the easiest thing on the slate, publish it the same week, and the pipeline gets tested at low stakes instead of on the flagship.

> **Read this first.** Both of these people are **independent contractors, not employees.** That is not a formality: it is lawyer question 5 in `outreach/08`, and getting it wrong means employment with back pay attached. **So this document sets deliverables and standards, never hours and supervision.** There is no clock-in. Nobody is told how to do their craft. What is agreed is what gets delivered, to what standard, by when, for how much. **If you find yourself directing someone's working day rather than reviewing their output, stop.**

## The morning: three hours, and only one of them is talking

### Hour 1. The three documents, read out loud, not emailed

**Read `standards.html` together, on a screen, all three of you.** Not as a formality. It is a public page making twelve checkable promises, and both of these people are about to be the ones keeping them.

Stop on these four, because they are the ones that change how someone works on the day:

| Promise | What it means at 2pm in a market |
|---|---|
| **The disclosure line is spoken on camera, in the same breath as the recommendation** | Not in the description. Not at the end. **If you name a business and Vaya earns from it, you say so while the camera is running.** Three exact lines are on the page. Learn them |
| **Everyone signs a release before we film** | Before the first frame. **No release, no footage used**, however good it is |
| **Every price on screen carries the date it was checked** | The videographer cannot fix this in the edit. It has to be said or shown on the day |
| **Sensitive stories get a second read by someone who was not behind the camera** | Health, family and money always count. **With three people, the second reader is whichever of you did not shoot it.** Name who that is before you start, not after |

Then `BRAND.md` for voice, and `kit/README.md` for the graphics and the pace rule. **Twenty minutes each, not an hour.**

### Hour 2. The one unresolved contradiction, settled today

`standards.html` currently promises both of these:

> "Everyone sees the finished piece before it goes out."

> "Nobody outside Vaya sees a piece before it publishes. Not partners, not the businesses in it, not anyone who pays us for anything."

**A restaurant owner who appears on camera is both of those people.** As written the page contradicts itself, and the first time a business asks to see the cut before it runs, you will be improvising a policy in front of them.

**The reading that holds, and the one to adopt:** a person sees **their own appearance**, not the piece. They get the clip they are in, to check they are not misrepresented and to exercise the blur or withhold choice the release gives them. **Nobody gets the edit, the running order, or anything that is not them.** A business gets nothing on the basis of being a business.

> **BOARD: Jordan confirms that reading and Claude amends `standards.html` to say it.** One line. It costs nothing today and it is a mess the first time it comes up live.

## The afternoon: shoot the easiest thing on the slate

**Do not shoot the flagship on day one.** `briefs/01-block-by-block.md` is two shoot days with time-of-day passes and it is the film everything else depends on. Shooting it cold on day one with a team that has never worked together is how you get a mediocre version of the most important thing you will make.

**Shoot a street-price piece instead.** One market, one dish or one product, the real price with the date said out loud, two angles, sixty seconds of clean street sound. It is a real asset, it uses every rule in the kit, and it is three hours.

| Time | What | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 13:00 | Walk the location together, no camera out | All three |
| 13:30 | **Releases signed by anyone who will be recognisable, before the camera comes out** | Correspondent asks, Jordan countersigns |
| 13:45 | Shoot. Two angles on every piece to camera. **Sixty seconds of clean street sound per location** | Videographer leads, and is not directed |
| 16:00 | **Card offload, both drives, before anyone leaves** | Videographer, Jordan watches once |
| 16:30 | Debrief: what broke, what to change tomorrow | All three |

**The videographer leads the shoot.** That is the deliverable they were engaged for. Jordan's job in the afternoon is to carry the releases and stay out of the frame.
