## Added 2026-09-13 (care migration)

| Date | Decision | By | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-09-13 | Care migration is a Vaya line, not a side product: the audience is the 45 to 60 year old adult child holding a US care bill, not the patient. Research in `research/care-migration.md`. | Jordan | The audience pass found the care-dependent household is the highest willingness-to-pay segment and is absent from the v2 slate. |
| 2026-09-13 | Any price quoted for 24-hour care is a two-person price. One worker cannot legally or humanly cover a 24-hour shift under RA 10361. | Claude recommended | Quoting a one-person number is the most common way this business is mis-sold and it collapses at month two. |
| 2026-09-13 | Vaya is never the clinical decision-maker. A licensed agency employs the worker, a Philippine physician directs care, and Vaya's written role is placement and coordination only. Professional indemnity cover before the first placement. | Claude recommended | The failure mode here is a harmed patient, not a bad review. |
| 2026-09-13 | The nurse's own episode ships in the same season as the cost comparison, and the worker's pay appears on screen in any film that compares care costs. | Claude recommended | Without it the series is a story about cheap labor. `standards.html` already commits Vaya to this publicly. |
| 2026-09-13 | Every placement runs through a certified worker under RA 11965 (TESDA NC II, a health degree, or a passed TESDA assessment). No exceptions, whatever the family asks for. | Claude recommended | An uncertified "caregiver" is a labor violation with the customer's name on it. Compliance through Placewell is the moat, not the overhead. |
| 2026-09-13 | **Glocal** is a strand, not a series: the theme of global money meeting local life, running across cities and seasons. Season one is The Cost of Care, episode one is Eight to One. Strands may be coined words; services still may not. Never register Glocal as a company or domain, Glocal Media already exists with Philippine offices. | Jordan | Jordan picked the name. Naming the strand rather than the series means it repeats in the next city with no new naming decision. |
| 2026-09-13 | Vaya's FDI line sells information and access, never capital: written briefings, ground checks, coordination of licensed partners, and Placewell's people. Flat fees only. Vaya never holds, routes, or takes a percentage of anyone's investment money. | Claude recommended | Transaction-based compensation is near-conclusive evidence of acting as an unregistered broker-dealer, and Exchange Act s.29(b) gives investors rescission rights when a deal sours. The downside is asymmetric and arrives at the worst moment. |
| 2026-09-13 | The FDI line is never named Vaya Capital, Vaya Invest, Vaya Ventures or Vaya Fund. Proposed: **Puhunan** as the strand, **Vaya Desk** as the service, **The Ground Check** as the flagship product. | Claude recommended, Jordan to confirm the service name | The name is a regulatory signal before it is a brand. A name that describes money management invites the reading you are trying to avoid. |
| 2026-09-13 | Puhunan the venture (own money into Filipino micro-entrepreneurs, three phases, in `jordan-projects/puhunan/`) is unchanged and stays separate from the FDI line. They share a strand name and nothing else. | Claude recommended | Opposite capital direction, 200x ticket size, different licence risk. Merging them would import Puhunan's lending exposure into a business that has none. |
| 2026-09-13 | Every published Philippine ownership, capital or tax rule carries the date it was checked and a citation. No exceptions. | Claude recommended | Foreign-ownership rules moved three times between 2021 and 2022 and the negative list is periodically reissued. This is the highest-liability content Vaya will publish. |
| 2026-09-13 | The three-way test governs the Vaya Desk menu: an American business gains, a regular American gains, and the Filipino side gains jobs at or above market with skills that stay. Two out of three is a no. The failing list is published, not just applied. | Claude recommended | A company that says what it will not sell is believable about what it will. Consistent with `standards.html`. |
| 2026-09-13 | Care training capacity ranks above care facilities on the Desk menu, and any facility project must have a training investment beside it. | Claude recommended | The Philippines faces a projected 196,000-nurse shortage and 56% of healthcare students never reach the workforce. Bidding for existing nurses is zero-sum against Filipino patients; growing the pool is not. Vaya must be able to answer this on camera. |
| 2026-09-13 | The retail-investor MVP is media-for-equity, not a fund. The Filipino company is the issuer and raises its own money under Reg D 506(c), which is open to foreign issuers. Vaya makes films, takes equity for delivered media, and is never in the money path. Rung one is plain paid production, starting now. | Jordan proposed, Claude confirmed against the rules | Reg CF requires a US-organized issuer but Reg D has no nationality requirement, so the company can raise directly and Vaya never has to be issuer, broker, fund or manager. |
| 2026-09-13 | Vaya may hold equity in a company it covers only if the grant is for delivered media, never contingent on a raise, disclosed on camera every time, and unconnected to any fee on capital raised. | Claude recommended | A contingent grant is transaction-based compensation in a costume, and promoting a security while holding an undisclosed interest is touting under Securities Act s.17(b). |
| 2026-09-13 | The target for an equity or investment story is a Filipino company raising roughly US$250k to 2M, not a micro-entrepreneur. Micro-deals stay with Puhunan and Jordan's own money. | Claude recommended | A single-company 506(c) costs an estimated US$10k to 25k in US counsel, which a PHP 300,000 raise cannot carry. |
| 2026-09-13 | Non-accredited Americans are reachable: a US holding company that actually owns the Philippine operating business can raise under Reg CF, and a first raise under about US$124,000 needs only self-certified financials. This corrects the earlier finding that Reg CF was simply closed. | Jordan pushed, Claude verified | Reg CF excludes a foreign-organized issuer, not a US parent holding a foreign subsidiary. The Delaware flip is the ordinary structure for international founders, not a workaround. |
| 2026-09-13 | The route choice (Reg CF with a flip, reaching everyone, versus 506(c) with no flip, reaching the accredited 18.5%) belongs to the Filipino company, not to Vaya. Vaya's position is identical either way: make the film, hold equity if granted, disclose it, take nothing connected to the raise. | Claude recommended | A flip means the founder gives up direct ownership of their own company and takes on US tax reporting. That is their call and many should refuse. |
| 2026-09-13 | The financing instrument follows the exit, not the excitement. Exit-track companies get equity or a SAFE; companies that will never be sold get revenue-share notes or debt; pre-revenue companies that will not be huge should not raise from retail Americans at all and should chase the DOST Startup Grant Fund, RA 11337 endorsement, local venture, and customers. | Claude recommended | Equity in a company that never exits pays no income ever, which is the opposite of the stated goal. A SAFE in such a company never converts. |
| 2026-09-13 | Vaya takes equity only from exit-track companies. From a company that will not exit, Vaya takes a capped revenue share for its media work instead. | Claude recommended, revises the 2026-09-13 media-for-equity decision | Vaya's equity in a non-exiting company is as worthless as the investors'. Most Filipino companies worth filming will never be acquired. |
| 2026-09-13 | The MVP of the whole investment thread is paid company films: cash only, ten of them, before any equity, revenue share, or raise conversation. Spec in `research/company-films-mvp.md`. | Claude recommended | It needs a camera and an email, not a lawyer, and it produces the revenue, portfolio, relationships and audience that every later rung depends on. |
| 2026-09-13 | Company selection filter: revenue now, a founder who will say real numbers on camera, something to look at, ability to pay PHP 60,000 to 150,000, and a constraint that is attention rather than capital. All five, or no film. | Claude recommended | The second condition disqualifies most candidates and must be asked first. |
| 2026-09-13 | Vaya Landing provisions a **native Globe or Smart eSIM**, not a physical SIM and not a travel eSIM. Travel eSIMs (Airalo, Holafly) are data only with no phone number, so they cannot receive the SMS one-time passwords that Maya, GCash and Grab require. Vaya says so honestly on camera and tells two-week visitors to just buy an Airalo. | Jordan asked, Claude verified | A +63 number is the whole point of the SIM step. A native eSIM delivers it with no plastic and no carrier-store queue, and can be registered before the guest lands. |
| 2026-09-13 | Landing and Visa Desk are sold as one story. A tourist SIM deactivates after about 30 days unless an approved visa extension is shown, and a dead SIM locks the wallet through the broken OTP chain. | Claude recommended | The strongest natural cross-sell in the service list, and it is a fact rather than a pitch. |
| 2026-09-14 | Seven product families, each carrying the Vaya name, with plain nouns inside: **Vaya Desk** (paperwork and government offices), **Vaya Concierge** (a person on the ground), **Vaya Care** (people employed in your home), **Vaya Property** (we look before you sign), **Vaya Tours**, **Vaya Business** (company market entry), **Vaya Studio** (films for other people). | 2026-09-14 | Vaya may make paid marketing films for licensed brokers and developers (Vaya Studio — Property), but never hosts a listing, never collects a buyer enquiry, and never takes anything contingent on a sale. | 2026-09-14 | The Rent Index is the destination product: building-level asking rent, real closing rent and vacancy, sold to brokers as a quarterly subscription. Data collection starts in the landing weeks; it sells in year two. | Claude recommended | Brokers pay for accuracy, so bias destroys what they bought, which makes it the only property revenue with no conflict built into it. This supersedes the earlier, over-cautious "never host a listing" rule. | Jordan pushed back, Claude verified and corrected | Lamudi and Property24 hold no broker licences and operate lawfully because representations are the broker's, not the platform's.
