# The Madrid Three · PRE01 to PRE03

**Shot in Madrid, on a phone, before the move. No crew, no budget, no Manila.**

These are not warm-ups. They are the test. Three films answer three questions nobody can answer for you from outside:

1. **Do you finish things?** You will know after the third one, not the thirtieth.
2. **Does anyone watch?** A real answer before a peso is spent on production.
3. **Do you like doing it?** The question that kills more projects than money does.

If you enjoy making these, hire the student in January and the whole plan works. If you hate it, you found out for free, fifteen months early, and Vaya becomes a services business with no films. That is also a real business, and a smaller one.

**Publish all three even if they are bad.** An unpublished film answers none of the three questions.

# PRE01 · Fifteen months out

**Sells nothing. This is the film that makes you a person instead of a brand.**

## Premise, one line
**"I am moving to Manila in January 2027. I am not on holiday, I am not retiring, and I have not figured it out yet."**

## The open, first 10 seconds
**You, talking to the camera, no title card, no music.** Say the date out loud.

> "January 2027. That is fifteen months. Here is what I still do not know."

## The beats
| # | Beat | What is on screen |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The decision, and when you made it | You, straight to camera |
| 2 | **Why Manila and not the twenty other places** | Say the real reason, including the unflattering part |
| 3 | What you are giving up in Madrid | Show the street, the flat, the routine |
| 4 | **The three things that scare you** | Name them. Do not resolve them |
| 5 | What you are doing about it between now and then | The actual list, on screen |
| 6 | The ask | "I am filming the whole thing. Follow it or do not" |

## Shot list
- You talking, one continuous take if you can hold it
- **Madrid B-roll you already walk past.** The metro, the market, your street at the hour you usually see it
- A calendar or a flight search with the date
- Nothing staged. If it needs setting up, cut it

## What not to do
- Do not pretend to know more than you do. **The whole premise is that you do not**
- No stock footage of Manila. You have not shot it yet, so do not borrow someone else's
- Do not sell anything. Not one link

## The end
**"Fifteen months. Let us see."**

## Practical
- **Half a day.** Most of it is B-roll on the way somewhere you were going anyway
- Target 5 to 7 minutes

# PRE02 · The visa I thought existed

**The desk-research version of EP02. Proves you do the work.**

## Premise, one line
**"There is a Philippine digital nomad visa. It was signed in April 2025. You still cannot apply for it."**

## The open, first 10 seconds
**Screen recording. A search for "Philippines digital nomad visa."** Scroll the results that all say it exists.

> "Every one of these is wrong."

## The beats
| # | Beat | What is on screen |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What the internet says | The search results, the headlines, the dates |
| 2 | **What the actual order says** | EO 86, April 2025. Read the relevant line on screen |
| 3 | **The portal with no application on it** | Screen record the official portal. Show the absence |
| 4 | So what can you actually do | 30 days on arrival, extendable toward 36 months |
| 5 | **The 59-day line and why it matters** | Past it you get an ACR I-Card, which unlocks a bank account. Draw the chain |
| 6 | What this costs, honestly | Every fee, dated, on screen |
| 7 | What you personally are going to do in January | Your own plan, said out loud |

## Shot list
- **Screen recordings, most of the film.** The search, the order, the portal, the fee schedule
- You reacting, in a small corner or between sections
- A printed page with the relevant line highlighted, on a desk

## The number rule
**Every fee and every date gets checked the week you publish.** Bureau of Immigration fees move. A wrong number is the thing people quote back at you forever.

**Say the date you checked, on screen.** "Correct as of [date]" is the most trustworthy thing in the film.

## What not to do
- Do not explain how to avoid a fee or overstay. **Ever**
- Do not claim Vaya files anything. You are in Madrid and nothing is signed
- Do not name the partner agency. Not signed yet

## The end
**"When I land in January I am filing this myself, on camera."** Points at EP02 without selling it.

## Practical
- **One day, entirely at a desk**
- Target 6 to 9 minutes. This one can run long because it is useful
- This film ranks. It answers a question thousands of people search and every result gets wrong

# PRE03 · I am renting a flat I have never seen

**The housing research film. Sets up Vaya Home without selling it.**

## Premise, one line
**"Makati scores 62 on safety. Manila as a whole scores 35. Every listing photo crops the difference."**

## The open, first 10 seconds
**Two listing photos side by side. Both look identical. Both are described the same way.**

> "One of these is in a neighbourhood I would live in. I cannot tell you which one from the photos, and neither can you."

## The beats
| # | Beat | What is on screen |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The two numbers | 62 and 35, on screen, with the source and date |
| 2 | **Why a city-level number is useless** | The district is the unit, not the city |
| 3 | Real listings, real asking prices | Scroll them. Say what each one is not telling you |
| 4 | **What the photos crop** | The lobby, the lift, the hour of day, the thing outside the frame |
| 5 | What you cannot know from Madrid | Be specific. Water pressure. Noise at 2am. Whether the lift works |
| 6 | **What you are going to do about it** | Your actual plan for January, including the part that might go wrong |

## Shot list
- **Screen recordings of real listings.** Blur or avoid the agent's name and contact
- The two safety numbers, big, with the source visible
- A map with the districts marked
- You, reacting to a listing in real time. Unscripted is better here

## The legal line
- **Do not name a broker, do not link a listing, do not take a referral from anyone.** You are commenting on public listings, not advertising property
- **Do not say a specific unit is good or bad value.** Talk about what you cannot tell, not about what the price should be
- The five-condition rule in `DECISIONS.md` applies when Vaya showcases a listing. **This film showcases nothing.** It is about the limits of looking

## What not to do
- No "hidden gem", no "up and coming". Banned words, and they are also lies you cannot verify from here
- Do not pick a winner. The point is that you cannot

## The end
**"In January I am going to look at five of these in person and film every one."** That is EP05 and you are not selling it yet.

## Practical
- **One day at a desk**
- Target 6 to 8 minutes
- **Verify both safety numbers and their date before you publish.** They are the spine of the film

# How to actually shoot these

## The gear, and the one thing that matters
**Audio. Not the camera.** People forgive a soft image and leave over bad sound.

| | What | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Camera** | The phone in your pocket, rear camera, 4K at 30fps | The rear camera is far better than the front one. Turn the phone around and use a mirror or the preview on a laptop |
| **Sound** | Wired earbuds with a mic, held or clipped near your chest. Or a clip-on lav, about 20 to 30 euros | **The built-in phone mic in a room is the single most common reason a film looks amateur** |
| **Light** | Sit facing a window. Never with the window behind you | Free, and better than most lights |
| **Stability** | Any surface. A stack of books works | Handheld talking-head reads as sloppy |

Before every take: **clean the lens**, lock focus and exposure by pressing and holding on your face, and check you are not in portrait mode.

## Screen recording
- **Mac:** Cmd + Shift + 5
- **iPhone:** add Screen Recording to Control Centre
- Record at the resolution you will publish. Zoom the browser to 150% first so text is readable on a phone

## Editing
**CapCut, free.** Good enough and fast. DaVinci Resolve is free and better if you want to learn it, but do not learn an editor and make your first film in the same week.

Rules that matter more than technique:
- **Cut the first eight seconds.** Whatever you shot first, the film starts later than you think
- One idea per cut
- **No music under talking.** If you want music, put it under B-roll only
- Subtitles on everything. CapCut does them automatically and most people watch muted

## Output per film
- **One long cut**, 5 to 9 minutes, for YouTube
- **Three vertical cuts**, 30 to 60 seconds each, pulled from the strongest moments. Not trailers. Each one stands alone
- Verticals go to TikTok, Reels and Shorts. Same file, three places

## The cadence
**One film every two weeks. Three films by the end of week six.**

Not one a week. You will miss it, feel bad, and stop.

## What to do with them
- Publish on YouTube under Vaya
- Post the verticals
- **One line in every description: the beehiiv signup**
- Then wait. Do not check the numbers for 48 hours

## The honest part
**The first one will be bad.** Not "modest", bad. You will watch it and want to delete it.

Publish it. The point is not the film, it is finding out whether you will make the second one.

Three films are published, each has three verticals out, each description carries the newsletter link, and you can say honestly whether you want to make the fourth.
