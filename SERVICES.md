## The insight that makes this small

**You do not set up nine services. You sign four suppliers.**

| Supplier | Unlocks |

| **One transport company** | Landing, Driver blocks, Day Trips, and the transport half of the Month |
| **One BI-accredited visa agency** | Visa Desk |
| **One licensed guide** | Food Tour, Vaya Nights |

Four emails. That is the whole supply side.

**And two services need no supplier at all**, which means you can sell them from Madrid this month.

## The three gaps worth adding

Confirmed by expat research: bureaucracy, connectivity and schooling come up constantly and are not on the current list.

| New service | Why | Supplier | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Connection** | Slow and inconsistent internet is the top remote-work complaint. Getting a proper line installed is a bureaucratic ordeal | An ISP contact, or fold into Landing | Low. Add to the Month |
| **School placement** | Corporate movers with children pay relocation firms well for this. High value, low volume | An education consultant | Medium. Wait for a first corporate client |
| **Household shipping referral** | Moving a household from the US runs US$2,500 to 8,500. Pure referral fee | An international mover | Low. One email, one commission agreement |

**Do not build any of these now.** Log them, and add the first one a real customer asks for.
